Open Duck Mini v2: pinouts

Every connection to the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, the servo bus and the power system, for this build.

Left and right are the duck's own sides. Facing the duck, its left is on your right. Pin numbers are physical header pins (1–40), not GPIO numbers. Pin 1 is the square pad, at the end nearest the microSD slot.

Raspberry Pi 40-pin header

5 V3.3 VGround I2C (IMU)I2S (speaker / mic)GPIO / PWM Unused

All ground pins (6, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34, 39) are connected together on the board, so any of them works. Grounds are spread out here so no pin gets more than one or two wires. Both 5 V pins (2, 4) are the same rail, fed by the UBEC.

By device

Power in: UBEC (5 V for the Pi)

UBEC outputPi pin
+5 V (red)45 V

Set the UBEC jumper to 5.0 V. Its input comes from the battery after the switch. The Pi is powered through these header pins, so the Pi's PWR IN micro-USB is left empty.

The I2C bus runs at 50 kHz (dtparam=i2c_arm_baudrate=50000 in /boot/firmware/config.txt) because of the Pi's clock-stretching bug with this chip.

Foot switches

SwitchPi pin
Left foot15GPIO 22
Right foot13GPIO 27
Common (both switches)14GND

The switch connects the pin to GND when the foot touches down. The Pi's internal pull-up holds it high otherwise, so no resistor is needed. Each foot has two switches: wire them in parallel (either one pressed counts as contact). Use the switch's COM and NO (normally open) tabs.

Ear servos (9 g micro servos)

Servo wireLeft earRight ear
Signal (orange / yellow)33 GPIO 1332 GPIO 12
+5 V (red)22 (shared)
GND (brown / black)3430

The signal pins must be GPIO 12 and 13: they are the Pi's hardware PWM outputs, which drive the ears without jitter. Splice the two red wires into one lead for pin 2, or use pin 4 alongside the UBEC.

The official assembly guide's table swaps left and right for the ears and the eyes. This sheet follows the code that actually runs on the duck. Both the official runtime and the version on your duck use: left ear = GPIO 13, right ear = GPIO 12, left eye = GPIO 24, right eye = GPIO 23. If an ear moves the wrong way once it's running, swap its two signal wires.

A GPIO pin gives 3.3 V and only about 16 mA. Put a series resistor (about 100 Ω) in each LED's lead unless the LED has one built in. Without it, the LED can damage the pin.

Camera (optional)

Ribbon cable into the Pi's CSI camera connector. A Pi Zero needs the narrow 22-pin Zero cable. Contacts face the board on the Pi end.

Servo adapter USB

Adapter USB-C → the Pi's micro-USB port marked USB (the one nearer the middle, not PWR IN). Plug it in before powering on, or it may not show up as /dev/ttyACM0.
