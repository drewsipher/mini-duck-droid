Old, best and new, let go

The leading hand, tried on the s3 brain before training with it

The hand is a horizontal spring from a point 10 cm above the trunk's centre of mass to a point that walks ahead at the commanded speed in the duck's own heading. It is capped at a fraction of body weight (the duck weighs 2.1 kg, so 10 % is 2.1 N), reaches its cap 3 cm behind the hand, and in these tests the hand never gets more than 8 cm ahead. It is drawn as the blue string. Everything below is the final s3 brain, which never trained with a hand, at real servo torque.

Why the second spring stays. A pull at the top of the trunk rights the duck only toward the hand, and the hand is ahead by design, so while it leads the lever-arm torque is a forward pitch, never a correction. A parent's hand also holds the child's arm up, which is an orientation constraint. The upright spring is that second half of the grip. It should be the last thing faded, not the first. Fading the hand first costs nothing, since the hand force is already zero whenever the duck keeps up.

Round 4 settings

Restored from the s2a checkpoint. Hand 3–10 % of weight, spring 3–6, roll bias 0–0.1 rad, sampled per episode. The hand may get 30 cm ahead; at 30 cm the episode ends, so a duck that stands on a forward command loses the alive reward instead of earning it. Real servo torque (1.6–2.1 N·m) from the start, pushes capped at 0.4 m/s, imitation reward off. 100 M steps.

Round 4 result

What went wrong. The drop was a cliff, not a slope. The s2a brain can manage 0.06 m/s at real torque, so on a 0.15 m/s command the hand dropped it after about three seconds no matter what it did. Walking and standing earned the same short episode, walking cost more in action rate and fall risk, and freezing won. Turning went with it. A reward that fell for 100 M steps also says the restored optimizer state did not cope with the changed episodes.

Rounds 5 and 6

Round 5 (h2) replaced the cliff with a dense cost of 5 reward points per metre of lead every step, the drop moved out to a metre, on the model servos. Round 6 (a2) kept that and let the two ankles move faster: a 20 Hz filter and 8 rad/s against 8 Hz and 4 rad/s for every other joint, with 30 % of the smoothness cost, on the measured servos. A probe before round 6 showed the s2a brain at real torque walks at 0.067 m/s held only by the spring, and 0.075 m/s with the faster ankles, so the servos are not what stops it walking unheld; balance is. Both rounds ended the same way as round 4: standing on every speed command from 0.05 to 0.2 m/s, even in the harness, with turning kept.

What three rounds say. The hand did what it was built to do: standing became expensive and the returns favoured walking. PPO still trained the stepping out, three times, with three different scorings. The thing all three lack, and attempt 3 had, is a reward that holds a stepping pattern in place while the rest is learned. In attempt 3 the imitation reward did that, and s2c recovered the walk after s2b lost it. With the reference gait gone there is no stepping prior at all, and a 0.06 m/s walk is fragile in the policy's parameters.

The candidate fix. A gait-clock foot-swing reward: the clock is already in the observation, and the reward asks only that each foot be off the floor during its half of the cycle and down during the other half, with no joint-angle target. That restores the prior without the hand-animated legs. Hand, spring, lead cost and the ankle settings stay as they are.

Round 7: the stride you puppeteered, as the reference

The generated gait is gone. The reference is now a stride recorded and edited in the puppet tool on the real duck: 1.72 s after trimming, both legs, looping, the left foot in swing 21 % of the cycle and the right 29 %. The imitation reward follows it whenever a forward speed above 0.03 m/s is commanded and asks for its standing frame otherwise. The legs were allowed ±0.5 rad from home instead of ±0.25, because the stride swings the hips 20 degrees past home. Held by a spring of 3–6 with up to 20 % lift, real servo torque, forward commands only, from the s2a checkpoint.

How the turning example was made

Rather than the generated grid, the turning reference for the next round is the smoothest turn any brain has produced: s2a's. A new recorder runs a brain in the simulator at real servo torque, lets it settle for three seconds, then averages its joint angles over 13 cycles of its gait clock into one 0.54 s loop. The result is saved in the puppet tool's clip format, so it also appears in the clip list there and can be played on the duck. The two clips are near mirrors of each other, which is a good sign that the recording is clean.

Alignment check. The training env scores each step's pose against the reference a few control steps later than the recorder stamps it (step order plus the env's action delay), so the recorder shifts its frames. Measured inside the env with the s2a brain held by a spring of 4 and turning left: a shift of 0 steps gives an imitation reward of 0.30, 1 gives 0.65, 2 gives 0.89, 3 gives 1.01, 4 gives 0.97. Three is the default. The same brain against the puppet stride scores −8.2, so the reward does tell the two apart. When several directions are commanded at once the turn example wins over walking and walking over side-stepping, and the gait clock's period follows the same blend.

One correction. The unheld scores and videos of the round 7 brain so far used the generated gait's 0.54 s clock, not the 1.72 s clock it was trained with. Re-scored on its own clock it still stands on a forward command and still falls in the speed test, so nothing changes in substance; the scorer now reads the reference settings and sets its clock to match.

What the curves say about attempt 3

What attempt 3 showed. The weight support stopped mattering after s2a: the s2a brain walked with no weight carried as long as any spring was present. The spring then came off in three steps and the walk survived down to 1 N·m/rad. What never transferred is recovery from the env's pushes (0.1–1.0 m/s every 5–10 s). With no spring a push ends the episode, and with 20 reward per step for staying alive against at most 2.5 for speed, the optimizer settles on stepping in place. The hand attacks that directly: standing still is now also a way to lose the episode.

The harness

Four forces on the duck, each sampled once per episode so one stage already contains the next stage's easiest cases. The policy never sees them; it feels them through the IMU and the joints.

Smoothing is constant and is a deployment setting: 8 Hz action low-pass, 4 rad/s motor speed limit, and a cost on action acceleration. The runtime must run the same low-pass (cutoff_frequency=8).
