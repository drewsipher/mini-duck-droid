# Making MuJoCo match a small servo-driven biped (sim-to-real / real-to-sim), as of 2026-11

Scope: Open Duck Mini v2 class robot (14 Feetech STS3215 7.4 V, position mode P-only, Pi Zero 2 W, 50 Hz Python loop, BNO055-class IMU, 2S Li-ion, TPU feet on wood), simulated in MuJoCo/MJX with Open Duck Playground / Brax PPO and a BAM-fitted position actuator (kp 17.11, forcerange ±3.23 N·m). Observed gaps the notes must explain: real servos ~1.48× stiffer than the model on a static scale test; real tracking error during a fast recovery step 2.1° mean / 7.9° max vs 1.4° / 4.9° in sim; quiet standing transfers, a learned recovery step amplifies tilt on hardware.

Convention: everything under "Cited Findings" is a documented number with a link. Everything under "Inferences" is an estimate, derivation, or recollection and is labelled as such. "From memory" means the fact was not re-verified in this session and should be checked before being relied on.

## Q1. Actuator modelling for hobby serial servos (BAM, actuator nets, PD + limits, backlash, registers, identification tests)

### Takeaway
The best-documented route for this servo class is Rhoban's BAM (ICRA 2025): a pendulum bench, a DC-motor voltage model with saturation, plus an extended friction model (M1 Coulomb-viscous through M6 load-dependent/Stribeck/quadratic) fitted with CMA-ES to ~100 logged 6 s trajectories; it halves-to-thirds the trajectory error versus plain Coulomb-viscous. For the STS3215 specifically, the single most important undocumented fact found is a factory acceleration cap (RAM register 85, factory value 50, ~6–28 rad/s² measured) that is not in BAM's STS3215 model and that silently slows fast steps while leaving slow motion and static stiffness untouched; that pattern matches the duck's "quiet standing fine, fast recovery step worse" symptom exactly.

### Cited Findings

BAM: what it is and how it is fitted
- BAM is a Rhoban project ("Extended Friction Models for the Physics Simulation of Servo Actuators", Duclusaud, Passault, Padois, Ly, ICRA 2025, pp. 12091–12097; arXiv 2410.08650). It is "an identification pipeline that fits friction models to recorded trajectories" and ships identified models for Dynamixel MX-64, MX-106, XL-320, XL330-M288-T, eRob80:50/100 and Feetech STS3215, with "a simple API to use these models in MuJoCo CPU and MuJoCo Warp" — [Rhoban/bam README](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam)
- The README states the Coulomb-Viscous model used in MuJoCo and IsaacGym "is too simplistic to accurately represent complex friction phenomena"; the extended models add Stribeck, load-dependence and quadratic effects — [Rhoban/bam README](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam)
- Test bench (paper): a rigid pendulum of length l with point mass m; Dynamixel runs used lengths 0.1, 0.15, 0.2 m and masses 0.5, 1, 1.5 kg; P gains 4, 8, 16, 32 (Dynamixel). Four trajectory families: (1) accelerated oscillations (constant amplitude, increasing frequency — i.e. a chirp), (2) slow oscillations with smaller faster sub-oscillations, (3) slow raise/lower with a static plateau, (4) lift-and-drop with motor current zeroed / H-bridge released so back-EMF is absent. About 100 logs of 6 s per actuator — [BAM paper v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4)
- Motor model (paper): voltage law U = clip(PID, [−U_max, U_max]), τ_m = (k_t/R)·U − (k_t²/R)·θ̇ (back-EMF gives the velocity-dependent torque limit); alternative current law with I clipped by the back-EMF-limited current and a heating limit; J = m·l² + J_m (armature); k_t, R, J_m identified when not known — [BAM paper v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4)
- Friction variants (paper, τ_f^m is the friction torque budget, all parameters positive): M1 = K_v|θ̇| + K_c (2 params, the MuJoCo default); M2 = M1 + Stribeck e^(−|θ̇/v_s|^α)·K_c^s (5 params); M3 = M1 + load-dependent K_l|τ_m − τ_e| (3 params); M4 = M3 + Stribeck with load term (7 params); M5 = directional load terms |K_m τ_m − K_e τ_e| plus Stribeck (9 params); M6 = M5 + quadratic term Q (11 params). Applied friction τ_f = clip(τ_f^stop, ±τ_f^m) with τ_f^stop = −(J/Δt·θ̇ + τ_m + τ_e) — [BAM paper v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4)
- Identification (paper): CMA-ES via Optuna, loss = mean absolute error between simulated and measured trajectories, 75 % identification / 25 % validation, three runs per model, converging in under 5 minutes and ~4000 iterations — [BAM paper v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4)
- Results (paper): MAE reduction of best model vs M1: MX-64 2.93×, MX-106 2.02×, eRob80:100 2.34×, eRob80:50 1.51×. For Dynamixel the gains stop at M5 (M6 adds nothing); on a 2R Dynamixel arm M4 was best with MAE "over twice as low" as Coulomb-Viscous across all trajectories and gains, and M5 overfit — [BAM paper v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4)
- MuJoCo integration (paper): MuJoCo only has Coulomb-viscous joint friction, so BAM "updates the static and viscous friction terms on-the-fly" each step from the extended model, using the previous step's external torque τ_e — [BAM paper v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4)

BAM on the STS3215 specifically (7.4 V vs 12 V, and the acceleration cap)
- A PR adding STS3215 12 V parameters fitted M1–M6 on 97 logs at P gains 4, 8, 16, 32 (kp 8 held out for validation), CMA-ES with 100k trials; pendulum arms 0.10 m (20 g) and 0.15 m (22.5 g) with tip masses 0.21, 0.41, 0.61 kg — [Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)
- Fitted M6 12 V relative to the shipped 7.4 V model: k_t ×1.77 (2.26 N·m/A), R ×1.51 (4.16 Ω), base friction ×2.8 (0.149 N·m), armature ×1.09, max velocity ×0.96, command delay ×1.12 ("nearly unchanged"). The author states the 7.4 V parameters "do not transfer to the 12 V variant" and the 7.4 V model would "predict the arm falling where the 12 V servo holds it by friction" — [Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)
- Held-out validation MAE (mrad, kp 8) for the 12 V STS3215: M1 20.8 overall (59.8 on lift-and-drop), M2 12.3, M3 12.0, M4 10.5, M5 10.3, M6 10.5; on sin_sin the range is 4.6–6.1 mrad — [Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)
- Acceleration cap: "Out of the box, the servo limits acceleration to about 10–28 rad/s² through an undocumented register" (register 85, "maximum acceleration", factory value 50); the STS3215 BAM model does not include it. Writing 85 = 0 disables it "but only after register 41 (acceleration) is also written"; both are RAM-only and reset at power-up. With the profile disabled the no-load response matched the 7.4 V reference, "about 110 rad/s²" — [Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)
- A second contributor (termvato) confirmed 85 = 0 disables it and needs a write to 41 to take effect; with the EEPROM lock off, 85 survives a power cycle. On a balanced rod cap 50 gave 6.0 rad/s² vs ~7.7 predicted (100 steps/s² × 50). On a bare horn one unit gave ~22 rad/s² at both cap 50 and 254 while a second unit gave 7.2 rad/s² at cap 50 ("unit-specific, cause unknown"); profile off gave 29–38 rad/s², "not ~110", suspected loop-limited at P=16. LeRobot writes 254 to both registers on every connect. Modelling the profile rather than disabling it, M4 on a held-out load gave 18.1 mrad mean error with the goal as sent vs 7.1 mrad when the goal was passed through the profile — [Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)
- The 12 V identification data is published as a release — [T-K-233/bam release](https://github.com/T-K-233/bam/releases/tag/sts3215-12v-bam-data-v1)
- A community Isaac port includes a pendulum testbench and a torch port of BAM's friction model with STS3215 identified models m1–m6 — [isaac_bam_actuators](https://github.com/SiIverHusky/isaac_bam_actuators)
- Pollen's own microduck_rl (mjlab) uses BAM on the Dynamixel XL330 with Coulomb, Stribeck and load-dependent friction, per-env domain randomization on friction magnitude, and a backlash variant "with backlash simulation and encoder feedback" — [pollen-robotics/microduck_rl](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck_rl)

STS3215 torque, backlash and registers
- Catalog rating: STS3215 19 kg·cm (= 1.86 N·m), Waveshare bus adapter, Pi Zero 2 W runtime — [orobot.io Open Duck Mini](https://orobot.io/o/program/BROKER-2/open-duck-mini)
- A community Open Duck Mini v2 build log reports the sim allows ±3.23 N·m per joint while "a real STS3215 stalls at 1.86 N·m", that the knee hit the sim ceiling 14–16 % of the time, and that retraining with the limit at 1.86 N·m raised walking speed under that limit from 0.057 to 0.130 m/s — [soyeon24/open-duck-mini-v2-build](https://github.com/soyeon24/open-duck-mini-v2-build) (note: 1.86 N·m is the catalog rating, not a measurement)
- Another port states its torque limit is roughly 70 % above the catalog rating and lists sim2real as "in progress" — [fport/open-duck-mac](https://github.com/fport/open-duck-mac)
- Backlash: one bench measured ~0.0151 rad (≈0.87°) against a datasheet limit of ≤0.5°; another found 1–2 mm of effective backlash at a 100 mm lever radius, increasing under load — [Robo9 STS3215 backlash/repeatability/torque test](https://robonine.com/testing-of-feetech-sts3215-servomotor-backlash-repeatability-and-torque/); [Robo9 backlash compensation](https://robonine.com/backlash-compensation-in-sts3215-servo-actuators/)
- Register map (community reference): P = 21, D = 22, I = 23 (1 byte each, defaults 32, 32, 0); EEPROM max torque limit = 16 (2 bytes, default 1000); RAM torque limit = 48 (0–1000 = 0–100 %); goal acceleration = 41 (1 byte, 0 = maximum, step ≈ 8.7°/s²); protection/holding torque = 34 (default 20 %); overload threshold = 36 (default 80 %); EEPROM writes require torque disabled first — [commanderfun/STS3215 REGISTER_REFERENCE](https://github.com/commanderfun/STS3215/blob/main/REGISTER_REFERENCE.md); [aifitlab STS memory table](https://wiki.aifitlab.com/feetech-servo-motor-docs/feetech-sts-servo-memory-table-analysis)
- Address 48 is LOCK on SCS servos but torque limit on STS — [circuitpython-ha-blinds issue #100](https://github.com/heikkileivo/circuitpython-ha-blinds/issues/100)
- Pollen's rustypot driver fixed register 85 ("maximum_acceleration") to one byte, noting the current Feetech table lists it as 1 byte and the older LeRobot table had guessed a size; the older memory table (V3.7, firmware 2.43) stops at address 69 — [pollen-robotics/rustypot PR #129](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/rustypot/pull/129)
- The Robo9 article describes the deadband as a tunable parameter around the target where a smaller deadband raises accuracy but can increase oscillation; no register address is given — [Robo9 backlash compensation](https://robonine.com/backlash-compensation-in-sts3215-servo-actuators/)
- The Open Duck HRI write-up says the team tunes servo acceleration and velocity profiles to smooth motion, acting as a low-pass filter against USB-serial jitter — [Hackster: HRI with the Open Duck Mini](https://www.hackster.io/PaulTR/hri-with-the-open-duck-mini-caa398)

Simple PD + DC-motor models and actuator networks
- Tan et al. 2018 (Minitaur): τ = K_t I, I = (V_pwm − V_emf)/R, V_emf = K_t q̇; because torque saturates with current they used a piecewise-linear torque-current lookup; position control is V_pwm = V·(k_p(q̄ − q_n) + k_d(q̇̄ − q̇_n)) using the current-step angle and velocity (not Bullet's implicit end-of-step constraint), with target velocity set to 0 after consulting the vendor's firmware. Validated with a sine trajectory on one motor. They state that with the implicit constraint "if large gains are used, the motors can remain stable in simulation but oscillate in reality" — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- Tan et al.: "inaccurate actuator models and lack of latency modeling are two major causes of the reality gap" — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- Hwangbo et al. 2019 actuator net: described in later surveys as a fully black-box neural-network actuator model used inside simulation for training, which "uses torque measurements for the target labels, which limits their method to robots that are equipped with torque sensors" — [Learning-based legged locomotion SoA survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01152); [Generalized actuator networks (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.09487)
- ToddlerBot (Dynamixel XC330/XC430/XM430/2XL430, 2M-baud TTL bus, 30 motors full-state feedback at 50 Hz): PD law with a k_d,min term "when the motor is active"; nine fitted parameters: damping, frictionloss, armature, τ_max, q̇ at τ_max, τ at q̇_max, q̇_max, k_d,min, τ_brake; damping/friction from back-driving at constant speed (linear fit: intercept = friction loss, slope = damping); armature from spin-up, power cut, energy integration I = E/ω²; actuation model fitted by commanding a chirp on the robot and optimizing position-tracking error. Reported "average tracking error of 1.3° with the optimized parameters", a passive-active ratio of 3 (~58 % gearbox efficiency), a Dynamixel-to-simulator kp conversion "of about 150", and that same-model motors "have nearly identical dynamics parameters" so a policy transferred to a second robot without re-identification. Example XC330: damping 0.0036 N·m·s/rad, armature 0.0040 kg·m², friction loss 0.036 N·m, τ_max 0.76 N·m, k_d,min 0.384, τ_brake 1.75 N·m — [ToddlerBot arXiv 2502.00893v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v3)
- PACE (ETH legged robotics) identifies actuator and joint dynamics "with standard joint encoders" using CMA-ES — [leggedrobotics/pace-sim2real](https://github.com/leggedrobotics/pace-sim2real)

### Inferences
- Estimate of the 7.4 V STS3215 BAM M6 values, back-derived from the 12 V ratios in PR #20: k_t ≈ 2.26/1.77 ≈ 1.28 N·m/A, R ≈ 4.16/1.51 ≈ 2.75 Ω, base friction ≈ 0.149/2.8 ≈ 0.053 N·m. These are derived, not read from the shipped JSON; read `bam` model files to confirm.
- The duck's "1.48× stiffer on a scale" is a static measurement and therefore includes stiction/holding friction, not just the P loop: at small deflection the base friction (~0.05 N·m estimate) plus the EEPROM "protection torque" behaviour can hold a load with little position error. A static scale test should be done at several deflections in both directions; the slope of torque vs deflection outside the hysteresis band is kp, the band width is friction/backlash. If the 1.48× persists at large deflection, scale the sim kp (the Playground already randomises kp ×1.1–1.8, which brackets 1.48 but centres the policy on the wrong value).
- Register 85 is the best single hypothesis for the recovery-step gap: a 6–28 rad/s² cap barely affects quiet standing or the 1.2 Hz-filtered walk, but a recovery step needs tens of rad/s²; the BAM STS3215 model (and hence kp 17.11 / ±3.23 N·m) was fitted with the profile off, so the sim is faster than the real servo in exactly the transients where the policy's tilt amplification appears. Tracking error being 1.5× higher on hardware (2.1° vs 1.4°) while static stiffness is higher is consistent with a rate/acceleration limit, not a gain error.
- The existing sim 4 rad/s target-rate clamp and 1.2 Hz action filter act on the command, not on the servo; they do not reproduce an acceleration-profile cap in the servo.
- Sim torque ceiling: ±3.23 N·m is 1.74× the 1.86 N·m catalog rating. In the BAM voltage model the electrical torque (k_t/R)·U_max at zero speed is the stall torque before friction; the net output after the fitted friction is lower and decreases linearly with speed (back-EMF). Whether 3.23 is the pre-friction stall or an overestimate cannot be resolved without a stall test (a scale on a lever arm, 2–3 s hold, at full battery and at 7.0 V).
- Position quantisation: 4096 steps/rev = 0.088°/step = 1.53 mrad; this is well below the 5–10 mrad MAE floor BAM achieves, so quantisation is not the fidelity limiter but should be added as rounding on the observation side (from the assignment's 4096 figure; arithmetic only).
- From memory (unverified this session): the STS memory table has CW/CCW "dead zone" registers at 26/27 (1 byte each, default 1 step) and a "return delay time" at 7 (unit 2 µs); goal-velocity mode and the "goal time/speed" fields in the position write define a trapezoidal profile together with register 41. Read these back on the duck before trusting them.
- From memory (unverified): Hwangbo et al. 2019 fed the net a short history of position errors and velocities (current, −10 ms, −20 ms) and output torque, trained on ~13 min of ANYdrive data; it is not applicable here because the STS3215 reports no torque, only a coarse "present load" PWM estimate.

### Gaps
- The shipped BAM STS3215 7.4 V parameter values (k_t, R, armature, delay, max torque, friction terms) and their validation MAE were not retrieved; only the 12 V ratios. The bam readthedocs theory page 404'd; the repo JSONs would have them.
- No documented torque-speed curve for the STS3215 7.4 V was found (only the 19 kg·cm catalog rating).
- No documented value for the STS3215 internal control-loop rate or the deadband register was found.
- Dynamixel XL430/XM430 BAM fits: not in the paper (MX-64/106, XL-320, XL330 only); not retrieved.
- Whether Open Duck Playground's kp 17.11 and ±3.23 N·m come directly from a BAM STS3215 fit, and which model variant (M1 vs M6) the Playground actually runs, was not confirmed.

## Q2. Latency: measuring and modelling the full loop on a Pi with a serial bus

### Takeaway
No documented end-to-end latency measurement for 14 STS3215 on one bus from a Pi Zero 2 W was found; what is documented is the range researchers randomise (0–40 ms, 3–20 ms control step in Tan 2018; 0–40 ms actuation and IMU latency in an Open Duck port), Tan's finding that unmodelled latency shrinks the real stability region enough to make a sim-stable feedback policy oscillate and fail, and BAM's fitted "command delay" term for the STS3215 (unchanged between 7.4 V and 12 V variants).

### Cited Findings
- Tan et al. define latency as the delay "between when a motor command is sent that causes the state of the robot to change and when the sensor measurement of this change is reported back to the controller"; Bullet applies commands and reports sensors instantaneously, which "makes the stability region of a feedback controller in simulation much larger than its implementation on hardware. For this reason, we often see a feedback policy learned in simulation starts to oscillate, diverge and ultimately fail in the real world" — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- Tan et al. randomisation ranges (Table I): mass 80–120 %, motor friction 0–0.05 N·m, inertia 50–150 %, motor strength 80–120 %, control step 3–20 ms, latency 0–40 ms, battery voltage 14.0–16.8 V, contact friction 0.5–1.25, IMU bias ±0.05 rad, IMU noise std 0–0.05 rad. They note "the control step and latency can fluctuate because of the non real-time system" — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- Minitaur loop: a TX2 runs the policy, an STM32 handles motors over UART; actions are sent to the microcontroller each step — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- An Open Duck Mini v2 MJX/Brax re-implementation tests push recovery with 0–40 ms of actuation and IMU latency — [SeanEZhou/openduck](https://github.com/SeanEZhou/openduck)
- BAM's STS3215 model has a "command delay" parameter; the 12 V fit changed it ×1.12 vs 7.4 V ("nearly unchanged") — [Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)
- ToddlerBot's only latency handling mentioned is the diffusion policy discarding 3 actions to compensate; its RL loop runs at 50 Hz with a 2M-baud bus — [ToddlerBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v3)
- ASAP's delta-action model and the 2026 "ActionDelta" model (per-joint MLP on the past three joint position/velocity observations, output a residual position target) absorb unmodelled dynamics including delay without explicit identification — [ASAP arXiv 2502.01143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01143); [Simulator Adaptation 2604.11090](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11090)

### Inferences
- Bus-time estimate (arithmetic, not a measurement): at 1 Mbaud, 10 bits/byte ≈ 10 µs/byte. A sync-write of 14 × (ID + 2 B position + 2 B time + 2 B speed) ≈ 100–110 bytes ≈ 1.1 ms. A sync-read of present position: a ~20-byte request plus 14 × ~8-byte replies ≈ 1.3 ms of wire time plus 14 firmware turnarounds and the servo "return delay" (from memory: register 7, unit 2 µs, factory often 0 on STS but 250 µs on Dynamixel). Python/pyserial on a Pi Zero 2 W typically adds several ms of syscall and scheduling overhead, so 3–8 ms for a full read+write cycle is a reasonable expectation; it must be measured.
- The IMU side: a BNO055 fusion output is a filtered estimate (see Q3); its lag plus I2C read time plus the 20 ms control period means an observation can lag the physical state by 1–2 control steps, which is exactly the 0–2 step IMU-delay range already randomised. The Playground's 0–2 step action delay (0–40 ms) matches Tan's range.
- Measuring the loop (method, not a finding): (a) timestamp each loop iteration with `time.perf_counter_ns()` and histogram the period over 10 min (mean, p99, deadline misses); (b) command a 20° step on one hanging leg joint and record the bus timestamp at which present-position first departs the start value (command→motion-onset delay) and when it reaches 63 % (rise time); (c) for the sensing path, flick the trunk while hanging and compare the time at which the present-position of a joint changes vs when the IMU gyro reports it, or film at 240 fps with a phone and align with the log using an LED toggled by the loop (phone slow-mo gives 4 ms resolution).
- Model as fixed delay + jitter: a fixed delay equal to the measured median (in whole 20 ms steps plus a sub-step fraction applied as a first-order hold) and a random extra 0–1 step per episode or per step; Tan's "control step 3–20 ms" randomisation also covers the case where the Pi misses a deadline and the step stretches.

### Gaps
- No paper found that isolates the policy-performance degradation vs unmodelled latency magnitude (an ablation curve) for legged RL; Tan 2018 reports the qualitative failure only.
- No measured numbers for STS3215 sync read/write round trips from a Pi, nor for the Open Duck Mini v2 runtime's loop jitter, were found.

## Q3. Sensing: IMU modelling (BNO055 fusion lag, noise, bias, mounting), projected gravity, joint reading noise

### Takeaway
Documented BNO055 behaviour relevant here is sparse but specific: its on-chip fusion can ignore slow rotations (~<3–5 °/s) in IMU mode, its fused gravity vector is a stable pitch/roll signal with no heading, and gyro bias dominates heading drift; Tan 2018 randomised IMU bias ±0.05 rad and noise 0–0.05 rad, and legged-robot estimation papers note bias estimation drifts when standing still. Mounting-offset calibration and projected-gravity construction are standard practice but no source with a numeric protocol for a small biped was found.

### Cited Findings
- In IMU mode (accel+gyro, no magnetometer) the BNO055's built-in fusion was reported to keep yaw static below about 3 °/s and only track rotation above 5–10 °/s, while the raw gyro stayed usable — [kriswiner/BNO055 issue #12](https://github.com/kriswiner/BNO055/issues/12)
- A Bosch representative (relayed on a forum) described the fused gravity vector as a stable pitch-and-roll representation with no heading information — [Raspberry Pi forum, BNO055 bias drift thread](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=206216)
- A practical calibration routine: wait for the calibration-status registers, then average stationary gyro Z readings and subtract the bias; integrated gyro bias is the main source of heading drift — [Daydream-Robotics Robot-Core issue #176](https://github.com/Daydream-Robotics/Robot-Core/issues/176)
- Multi-IMU legged-robot work reports that bias estimation can drift when the robot stands still with colinear contacts, leading to wrong orientation estimates — [Multi-IMU Sensor Fusion for Legged Robots](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11447)
- Tan et al. randomised IMU bias in [−0.05, 0.05] rad and IMU noise std in [0, 0.05] rad, and used only roll, pitch and the two angular rates (plus motor angles) as observations because other measurements "drift quickly" — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- The 2026 simulator-adaptation paper identifies from proprioception only (joint positions, velocities, actions) because base pose/velocity "are unavailable" on hardware; final positions were measured by hand to ±0.05 m — [Simulator Adaptation 2604.11090](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11090)

### Inferences
- Projected gravity is the unit gravity vector expressed in the trunk frame: g_body = R_world→body · [0, 0, −1]. It needs (a) an orientation estimate (quaternion from the BNO055, or the chip's own gravity-vector register) and (b) the rotation from the IMU frame to the trunk/pelvis frame used by the MJCF. If the chip's fusion deadband ignores slow tilts, the projected gravity will lag or freeze during slow lean — a plausible contributor to balance errors; using raw gyro + accelerometer with a complementary/Madgwick filter in the 50 Hz loop gives known, tunable lag (from reasoning; the deadband itself is the cited finding above).
- Mounting calibration (method): stand the robot on a level surface in the sim's reference pose and record the gravity vector for 10 s; the rotation that maps the mean vector to [0,0,−1] is the pitch/roll offset. Yaw-about-gravity of the IMU frame cannot be seen from gravity alone; obtain it by rolling the robot about a known trunk axis (e.g. tipping it sideways onto a fixed block) and matching the gyro axis to the trunk axis. Verify in sim by replaying the same tilt (see Q6).
- Sensor noise in sim: use measured std at 50 Hz from a 60 s static log (gyro and the gravity vector components) rather than guessed ranges; Tan's 0.05 rad noise std (2.9°) is large for a BNO055's fused output but is a reasonable randomisation upper bound (estimate).
- Joint readings: present position is quantised to 1.53 mrad; noise beyond that is mostly backlash (~0.9° measured, see Q1) between the load and the output encoder, which is a mechanical effect, not sensor noise (inference from the Robo9 numbers).

### Gaps
- BNO055 datasheet values (fusion output data rate, gyro noise density, bias stability, fusion latency) were not retrieved this session; from memory the fusion data rate is 100 Hz, but confirm.
- No legged-robot paper comparing BNO055 output against a reference IMU was found.

## Q4. Mass, CoM and inertia measurement; battery sag

### Takeaway
Tan 2018 is the clearest precedent: disassemble, weigh each link, find each link's CoM, compute inertia from shape with uniform density, then randomise mass ±20 % but inertia ±50 % precisely because inertia was only estimated; their battery randomisation spanned 14.0–16.8 V on a 4S pack. The duck's 2.107 kg model vs 1.5–1.8 kg estimate is a 17–40 % discrepancy, outside the ±10 % link-mass randomisation currently used.

### Cited Findings
- Tan et al.: "We disassemble a Minitaur, measure the dimension, weigh the mass, find the center of mass of each link and incorporate this information into the URDF file. Measuring inertia is difficult. Instead, we estimate it for each link given its shape and mass, assuming uniform density. We also design experiments to measure motor frictions." Mass randomised 80–120 % ("conservative ranges since we have measured them"), inertia 50–150 % ("less certain about inertia because it is estimated based on a uniform density assumption"), battery 14.0–16.8 V — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- The torque-sensor-less gear-driven humanoid work excites the robot with its position-controlled loop and fits body inertia and actuator parameters by minimising the difference between real and simulated sensor data using sampling-based black-box optimisation — [arXiv 2204.03897](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.03897)
- The 2026 simulator-adaptation paper does not tune mass at all; mass, PD gains and floor friction are only randomised during PPO — [Simulator Adaptation 2604.11090](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11090)
- ToddlerBot names motor temperature (not mass) as the main unmodelled factor: the actuation model "does not consider motor temperature and is less accurate when close to the performance limit", and endurance tests showed rising temperature pushing the robot out of the training distribution — [ToddlerBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v3)
- Battery-voltage torque scaling in the BAM/Tan motor model: τ_m = (k_t/R)·U − (k_t²/R)·θ̇, i.e. stall torque and no-load speed both scale linearly with supply voltage — [BAM paper v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4); [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)

### Inferences
- Total mass is the cheapest, highest-value measurement (kitchen scale, 1 minute). If the robot is 1.65 kg and the model 2.107 kg, the model is 28 % heavy: gravity torque at the hips/knees in a lean is 28 % too high in sim, so a policy trained there expects to need more torque than reality and, with the real servo also 1.48× stiffer, will over-correct — consistent with tilt amplification on hardware (inference).
- Per-link mass: weigh sub-assemblies at the servo horns (each STS3215 is ~55 g from memory, unverified); weigh the trunk with battery and Pi separately. Expect the trunk (battery + electronics + head) to dominate.
- CoM: balance the assembled robot on a thin edge (ruler) in the sagittal and frontal planes; the balance line in two orientations fixes x and y; for height, hang it from a string at two points and photograph the plumb lines (phone camera). The MJCF trunk CoM ±5 cm randomisation is large relative to a ~40 cm robot; centring it on a measured value and shrinking the range to ±1–2 cm would sharpen the policy (estimate).
- Inertia: bifilar pendulum, I = m·g·d²·T² / (16·π²·L) (d = string separation, L = string length, T = period), from memory — verify the formula before use. For a robot this small, CAD inertia with per-link measured masses (scale densities so CAD mass = measured mass) is usually adequate, and Tan's ±50 % inertia randomisation is the honest way to cover the residual.
- Battery sag: a 2S Li-ion runs ~8.4 V full to ~6.4 V near empty; with torque ∝ U, a servo fitted at 7.4 V delivers ~1.14× at 8.4 V and ~0.86× at 6.4 V (estimate). The Playground's torque randomisation 1.6–2.1 N·m already spans ±14 % around 1.85 N·m, which matches this; log pack voltage in every hardware trial so results can be binned by voltage.

### Gaps
- No source giving numeric sensitivity of balance to CoM error for a small biped was found.
- No measured STS3215 torque-vs-voltage curve was found (only the model's linear scaling).

## Q5. Contact: foot friction, TPU sole compliance, MuJoCo contact parameters

### Takeaway
Documented practice is thin: Tan 2018 randomised contact friction 0.5–1.25; the 2026 Go2 paper randomises floor friction rather than identifying it; the Open Duck DR uses 0.5–1.0. No source was found that tunes MuJoCo solref/solimp to a drop test for a small biped, so the procedure below is an inference built on standard physics.

### Cited Findings
- Tan et al. randomised contact friction coefficient in [0.5, 1.25] — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- The 2026 Go2/biped simulator-adaptation work randomises floor friction during PPO training but does not identify it; its identified "FricArm" parameters are joint friction (bounded [0, 0.5]) and armature — [Simulator Adaptation 2604.11090](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11090)
- PolySim argues for randomising across multiple simulators' dynamics (including contact models) to bridge humanoid sim-to-real — [PolySim arXiv 2510.01708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01708)
- ToddlerBot models spur gears with equality constraints, bevel gears with fixed tendons and linkages with weld constraints and says this "has empirically shown a small sim2real gap" without a number — [ToddlerBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v3)

### Inferences
- Tilt test for the coefficient (standard physics): put one TPU foot loaded with ~0.8 kg (half the robot) on the actual wood floor board, raise one end until it slips, μ_s = tan θ. Repeat 5× and on the dusty vs wiped board. A phone inclinometer gives θ to ~0.5°. Expect TPU on smooth wood to land in 0.5–1.0; if measured μ is e.g. 0.8 ± 0.1, narrow the DR to 0.6–1.0 centred on it.
- Compliance: TPU soles under 1.7 kg compress a fraction of a mm; MuJoCo's soft contact (solref time constant, from memory default 0.02 s and damping ratio 1; solimp default 0.9 0.95 0.001 0.5 2; condim 3 default — all from memory, verify in the MuJoCo XML reference) can be tuned by a drop-and-settle test: drop the standing robot from 2–3 cm, film at 240 fps, and match the settle time and bounce count in sim by adjusting solref's time constant. The 2 ms physics step bounds how short the time constant can be (solref timeconst should be ≥ 2× the step).
- Slip test: push the standing robot sideways slowly with a kitchen scale until a foot slides; the reading at slip onset / normal load is a second μ estimate and also a direct check of the sim's lateral push response (ties into Q6's standing push protocol).
- condim 4 (adds torsional friction) matters for a flat TPU sole when the robot pivots on one foot; worth testing because yaw drift in place is a common sim-real mismatch (inference).

### Gaps
- No documented MuJoCo solref/solimp values tuned to a measured drop test for any small biped were found.
- No TPU-on-wood friction coefficient measurement was found.

## Q6. Verification protocols and system identification (replay, ASAP, CMA-ES, standard tests, metrics, DR evidence)

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 literature converges on: log real rollouts, replay or match them in sim, and either (a) fit a handful of physical parameters with CMA-ES (BAM, PACE, SPI-Active, the 2026 proprioceptive-matching paper) or (b) learn a small residual (ASAP's delta-action, the 2026 ActionDelta/ResidAct) that makes sim match real, then fine-tune the policy in the corrected sim. Numeric "match" targets reported: 5–10 mrad MAE on servo trajectories (BAM), 1.3° average tracking error (ToddlerBot), <10 % parameter error under noise (2026 paper), and final-position drift reduced from ~1.2 m to ~0.2 m after fine-tuning (2026 biped). On DR, the evidence is that identified values plus narrow randomisation beats wide blind randomisation (Tan's reasoning; ASAP beats DR and SysID; the 2026 paper argues DR trades robustness for performance).

### Cited Findings
- ASAP (RSS 2025): stage 1 pretrains motion-tracking policies in sim; stage 2 runs them on the real robot, records data, trains a delta action model that "outputs corrective actions that are added to the recorded real-world actions", freezes it inside the simulator, fine-tunes the policy, and deploys without the delta model. Reported to reduce tracking error versus SysID, DR and delta-dynamics baselines — [ASAP arXiv 2502.01143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01143); [project page](https://agile.human2humanoid.com/)
- A follow-up trains a separate delta model per motion cluster rather than one unified model and reports better sim-to-real across diverse motions — [From Experts to a Generalist, arXiv 2506.12779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12779)
- Simulator adaptation via proprioceptive distribution matching (2026, Go2 + biped): hardware data = 64 trajectories × 4 s (~256 s, "less than five minutes") from the baseline policy under random velocity commands, proprioception only. Objective = average per-feature per-joint 1D Wasserstein distance over joint positions, velocities and actions, normalised by hardware statistics; temporal ordering is discarded so no time alignment or resets are needed. CMA-ES (cmaes library): FricArm with initial covariance 0.1 (friction)/0.05 (armature), 200 iterations; ActionDelta/ResidAct with covariance 0.25, bounds [−1,1], 10,000 iterations; each cost evaluation = 64 fresh 4 s rollouts. Sim-to-sim recovery: hip friction 0.21 vs true 0.2, calf armature 0.0741 vs 0.075. Under observation noise σ = 1.0/2.5/5.0 the Wasserstein cost gave 7.97 %/8.34 %/18.75 % parameter error vs 15.2 %/40.7 %/86.3 % for time-aligned observation matching. Sim-to-real biped: X drift 1.2 m → 0.2 m after fine-tuning; quadruped spring-joint drift ~2.18 m → ~0.49 m worst case. Static friction+armature "failed on the spring joint"; the residual actuator model gave the best fine-tuned policies — [Simulator Adaptation 2604.11090](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11090)
- SPI-Active: sampling-based identification of legged-robot physical parameters by matching simulated and real rollouts without differentiable simulators, plus an active-exploration stage that optimises commands to make data informative; reports 42–63 % gains over baselines in locomotion tasks — [SPI-Active project page](https://lecar-lab.github.io/spi-active_/)
- PACE: systematic sim-to-real for legged robots "identifying actuator and joint dynamics with standard joint encoders", CMA-ES — [leggedrobotics/pace-sim2real](https://github.com/leggedrobotics/pace-sim2real)
- A 2025 survey of sim-to-real for DRL bipedal locomotion treats offline system identification with excitation trajectories and residual dynamics learning as complementary approaches — [arXiv 2511.06465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.06465)
- BAM's identification metric: MAE between simulated and measured pendulum trajectories; 75/25 split; held-out gain validation in the STS3215 PR (kp 8 held out; 10.3–10.5 mrad for M4–M6 vs 20.8 mrad for M1) — [BAM paper v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4); [Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)
- ToddlerBot: fitted actuator "average tracking error of 1.3°"; walking tracking errors position 0.082 m (sim) vs 0.133 ± 0.018 m (real), linear velocity 0.016 vs 0.032 ± 0.002 m/s, angular velocity 0.056 vs 0.113 ± 0.010 rad/s; "the sim-to-real gap is notably smaller than the tracking gap" — [ToddlerBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v3)
- Tan et al. on DR: "dynamics randomization trades optimality for robustness", so they chose narrow ranges for measured quantities (mass, motor friction) and wide ranges only for uncertain ones (inertia) and for quantities that drift (motor strength, control step, latency, battery) — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- The 2026 paper argues DR "is limited to static parameters and trades off robustness against performance" and runs no DR baseline; it also reports no direct ASAP experiment, only that its time-aligned MatchS(20) baseline "corresponds to" ASAP's approach — [Simulator Adaptation 2604.11090](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11090)
- Athletic loco-manipulation sim-to-real (2025) is a further reference on closing the gap for dynamic skills — [arXiv 2502.10894](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10894)

### Inferences
- The open-loop replay test the engineer proposes is exactly BAM's and the 2026 paper's data source, but with the whole robot instead of a pendulum. Protocol (method): log at 50 Hz the commanded targets, present positions, IMU gravity vector and gyro, pack voltage, and loop timestamps for (a) 30 s quiet stand, (b) a 0.2 Hz squat, (c) a 1 Hz sine on one hip and one knee with the robot hanging, (d) the failing recovery step. Replay the logged targets open-loop in MuJoCo from the same initial joint pose and compare: per-joint MAE and max error (rad), per-joint peak velocity and acceleration, IMU tilt trajectory (deg) and its timing, and foot-contact timing. A hanging-leg sine test isolates the actuator from contact and mass.
- "Matches" numerically, from the cited precedents: per-joint trajectory MAE ≤ 10 mrad (0.6°) on slow/hanging motions and ≤ 20 mrad on fast steps (BAM M4–M6 vs M1 levels); the sim's recovery-step tracking error should reproduce the real 2.1° mean / 7.9° max within ~20 %, not 1.4°/4.9°; tilt-response timing (time to peak and settle after a push) within one control step (20 ms) and peak amplitude within 10–15 % (estimate; no paper states tilt tolerances).
- Order of fitting on the duck: first fix what is measurable (mass, CoM, friction coefficient, registers), then fit the actuator (acceleration cap, delay, kp, friction) on hanging-leg sine/step/chirp data with CMA-ES on MAE (a few hundred evaluations of a 6 s MJX rollout is minutes), then verify on the recovery-step replay. Only if a residual remains, train an ASAP-style delta-action MLP on the replay residuals (the 2026 paper's per-joint [8,4] MLP is tiny and trainable on minutes of data).
- DR after identification: Tan's logic applied to the duck — narrow kp to ±15 % around the fitted value instead of ×1.1–1.8; narrow link masses around measured values; keep latency 0–2 steps, torque 1.6–2.1 N·m (battery), friction 0.5–1.0 until measured. The current kp ×1.1–1.8 range contains the 1.48× factor but is centred at 1.45×, so if the sim kp is simply wrong by 1.48 the policy is already "seeing" the real value mid-range; that the recovery step still fails suggests the gap is not kp alone.

### Gaps
- ASAP's quantitative tracking-error numbers and its G1 delta-model architecture were not extracted (only the abstract-level claim).
- Bjelonic et al. 2025 "Towards bridging the gap: systematic sim-to-real transfer for diverse legged robots" (the PACE paper) full text not retrieved; its reported tolerances are unknown.
- No standardised sim-vs-real test suite with published tolerances (sine sweep / drop / step / push / hold) was found; the protocol above is assembled from the sources' individual tests.
- "Learning to walk in minutes"/legged_gym lessons, RMA/privileged adaptation vs identification: not researched this session (no calls left); treat as unverified background.

## Q7. Small-biped precedents and what each reported as the dominant gap

### Takeaway
Among retrievable sources: ToddlerBot (Dynamixel, 50 Hz) did zero-point calibration plus per-motor-model sysID on a torque test stand and reports the actuator model (temperature, near-limit behaviour) as the remaining limitation; Open Duck community ports flag the torque ceiling (sim ±3.23 N·m vs 1.86 N·m catalog) and test 0–40 ms latency; Pollen's microduck uses BAM friction + backlash on XL330; Berkeley Humanoid Lite claims a small gap from custom actuators but publishes no sim-to-real numbers; Tan's Minitaur named actuator model and latency as the two dominant causes. Disney BD-X, K-Scale Zeroth and Unitree G1 papers were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- ToddlerBot: zero-point calibration via 3D-printed plug-in jigs (arm, neck, hip, ankle) because Dynamixels have no absolute zero, "within a minute"; motor sysID on a torque-sensed stand (secondary driving motor, 5 N·m powder brake, 3e-4 N·m precision); chirp fit on the robot; 1.3° average tracking error; a second robot ran the policy without re-identification; dominant remaining issue = actuator model vs temperature and near-limit operation — [ToddlerBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v3); [PMLR page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/shi25a.html)
- Berkeley Humanoid Lite attributes its "narrow sim-to-real gap" to custom actuators and integrated design; a critical review notes "no commanded-versus-actual velocity, no trial count, no fall rate, no continuous walking duration, and no description of domain randomization or sim-to-real calibration" — [Pith review of arXiv 2504.17249](https://pith.science/paper/2504.17249); [paper (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395364137_Demonstrating_Berkeley_Humanoid_Lite_An_Open-source_Accessible_and_Customizable_3D-printed_Humanoid_Robot)
- Open Duck Mini v2 community: torque ceiling mismatch and retraining result (Q1); 0–40 ms actuation/IMU latency push-recovery test with "0 % falls with randomization, noise and latency in simulation" (sim-only) — [soyeon24 build log](https://github.com/soyeon24/open-duck-mini-v2-build); [SeanEZhou/openduck](https://github.com/SeanEZhou/openduck)
- Open Duck Mini v2 hardware as built by the community: 40 cm, 14 × STS3215, Pi Zero 2 W, BNO055, MuJoCo RL exported to ONNX, "verified stable walking and disturbance resistance" — [Threads post (anecdotal)](https://www.threads.com/@dingcheng.h/post/DW_VJrrD-9p/)
- Pollen microduck (Dynamixel XL330, mjlab): BAM Coulomb/Stribeck/load-dependent friction, per-env friction randomisation, backlash variant with encoder feedback — [pollen-robotics/microduck_rl](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck_rl)
- Tan et al. (Minitaur, direct-drive, position mode): actuator model + latency were the two major causes; after modelling both and randomising Table I, trotting and galloping transferred — [Tan et al. RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)
- Torque-sensor-less gear-driven humanoid: excitation via the position loop, black-box optimisation of inertia and actuator parameters against real sensor data — [arXiv 2204.03897](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.03897)

### Inferences
- Across these, the recurring dominant gap for position-controlled hobby/gear actuators is the actuator (torque ceiling, friction, acceleration/velocity limits, temperature) with latency second; mass/contact appear mainly as randomisation ranges rather than as identified culprits. This matches the duck's symptom profile (static OK, transients wrong).
- From memory (unverified): Disney's BD-X paper (Grandia et al., "Design and Control of a Bipedal Robotic Character", RSS 2024) trained in Isaac Gym with PD joint-target policies and reported that modelling actuator torque/velocity limits and adding a first-order filter on targets were needed; I could not retrieve it to confirm any number, so treat as hearsay.

### Gaps
- Disney BD-X RSS 2024, Mini-BDX, K-Scale Zeroth, Pollen Reachy, and Unitree G1 RL sim-to-real details were not retrieved (search did not surface them within the call budget).
- Open Duck Mini v2's own maintainer notes on which gap dominated were not found; only community ports.

## A measurement and verification plan for the duck

Ordered by expected sim-fidelity gained per hour of builder time. Equipment in brackets. "Documented" items cite the sources above; everything else is an estimate.

- 0. (15 min, no equipment) Dump every register of all 14 servos at boot and after the runtime's init: P/I/D (21/22/23), goal acceleration (41), maximum acceleration (85), torque limit (48) and EEPROM max torque (16), protection torque (34), overload (36), return delay (7), min/max angle limits, and the dead-zone bytes. Record what the runtime writes. Documented: 85 factory 50 caps acceleration at ~6–28 rad/s² and is only disabled by writing 85 = 0 after 41 ([PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)); LeRobot writes 254 to both on connect ([same](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)). Decide: disable the profile (then the BAM model applies) or model it in sim (a 6–28 rad/s² acceleration limiter on the target, as termvato did, which cut held-out error from 18.1 to 7.1 mrad).
- 1. (10 min) [kitchen scale] Total mass with battery; then trunk+head, each full leg, and one bare servo. Update the MJCF so modelled total = measured ±2 %. Documented precedent: Tan weighed every link and then randomised mass only ±20 %. The 2.107 vs 1.5–1.8 kg discrepancy must be closed before any actuator fitting, because gravity torque sets what "stiff enough" means.
- 2. (30 min) [ruler edge, string, phone camera] CoM: balance on an edge in two planes for x,y; hang from two points with a plumb line for z. Centre the trunk-CoM randomisation on the measurement and shrink it from ±5 cm to ±1–2 cm (estimate).
- 3. (1 h) [the servos' own present-position reads, the Pi, pack voltage logger] Hanging-leg actuator tests at P=30 and P=8: steps of 10°, 30°, 60° (both directions), a 1 Hz sine ±20°, a 0.2–3 Hz chirp ±15°, each at full and at ~7.0 V battery. Log goal, present position, loop time, voltage at 50 Hz (and once at the fastest the bus allows). Extract rise time, peak velocity, peak acceleration, overshoot, steady-state error. Replay the same targets in MJX with the current actuator and compare MAE per trajectory; target ≤ 10 mrad on sine, ≤ 20 mrad on steps (BAM levels, documented). If peak acceleration on hardware is ≤ 30 rad/s² the register-85 cap is active.
- 4. (1 h) [scale on a lever arm] Static stiffness and stall: torque vs commanded deflection at 2°, 5°, 10°, 20° in both directions, hold 2 s each; the slope outside the hysteresis band is kp, the band is friction+backlash (expect ~0.9° backlash, documented by Robo9), and a 20 s hold at a fixed torque gives the holding/stall torque and reveals the protection-torque fold-back (register 34 default 20 %, documented). Compare to the sim's kp 17.11 and ±3.23 N·m; the sim ceiling is 1.74× the 1.86 N·m catalog rating (documented numbers, interpretation is an estimate).
- 5. (1 h) [Pi timestamps, one LED, phone at 240 fps] Loop latency: histogram the 50 Hz loop period over 10 min (mean, p99, misses); command→motion-onset delay on one joint from bus timestamps; IMU-path delay by flicking the hanging trunk and comparing gyro onset vs encoder onset, cross-checked with the camera. Set the sim's fixed delay to the median and keep ±1 step jitter (Tan: 0–40 ms, documented).
- 6. (45 min) [level surface, a wooden wedge of known angle] IMU: 60 s static log for gyro bias and noise std at 50 Hz; mounting offset from the mean gravity vector in the reference pose; slow-tilt test (1 °/s) to see whether the on-chip fusion freezes (documented deadband ~3–5 °/s in IMU mode); if it does, switch to raw gyro+accel with your own filter so the lag is known. Put the measured bias/noise into the sim's sensor-noise ranges.
- 7. (30 min) [tilt board, phone inclinometer, 0.8 kg weight] Foot friction: μ_s = tan θ at slip on the real floor, 5 repeats, clean and dusty. Narrow the sim's 0.5–1.0 range around it. Then a 2–3 cm drop-and-settle filmed at 240 fps; tune solref so bounce count and settle time match (estimate; no documented values).
- 8. (2 h) [all of the above logs] Open-loop replay gate: replay the hanging sine/step/chirp logs, then the quiet stand, the squat, and the failing recovery step, from the logged initial pose. Pass criteria: per-joint MAE ≤ 10/20 mrad (slow/fast), sim tracking error on the recovery step reproduces the hardware 2.1° mean / 7.9° max within ~20 %, and the IMU tilt trace matches in timing to within one 20 ms step and in amplitude within ~15 % (tolerances are estimates grounded in the BAM and ToddlerBot numbers). If this fails, run CMA-ES on (acceleration cap, command delay, kp scale, Coulomb/viscous friction, armature) against the hanging logs — the BAM recipe, documented — before touching anything else.
- 9. (optional, 2 h) [one spare servo, 10/15 cm arm, 0.2–0.6 kg masses] A BAM pendulum run at P = 8/16/32 for the actual 7.4 V units at the actual battery voltage, using Rhoban's pipeline; compare the fitted M4/M6 with the Playground's kp 17.11 / ±3.23 N·m. Documented rig and settings in PR #20.
- 10. (then) Retrain with DR centred on identified values (kp ±15 %, masses ±10 % around measured, friction ± measured spread), keep wide ranges only for what drifts (battery/torque 1.6–2.1 N·m, latency 0–2 steps, temperature). Re-run the recovery step. If a residual remains, fit an ASAP-style per-joint delta-action MLP on the replay residuals and fine-tune in the corrected sim (documented method; expect minutes of data to suffice per the 2026 paper's 256 s).
- 11. Standing push battery on hardware vs sim: calibrated pushes (a known mass on a string, or the scale) at 3 magnitudes and 2 directions, compare tilt peak and settle; this is the real-world counterpart of the existing `sim/fusion/runs/passive_push` sweep and should be the last gate before training any new recovery skill.
