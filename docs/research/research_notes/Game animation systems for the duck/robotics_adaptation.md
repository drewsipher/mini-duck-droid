# Game-animation concepts adapted to real legged robots, and layered control for small position-controlled bipeds (as of 2026-11)

Scope note: this covers only the robotics side (what has been built, what ran on hardware, what the evidence says). Sources are marked **[HW]** when the result was demonstrated on a physical robot and **[SIM]** when simulation-only. Local primary sources (the Open Duck code checked into this repo) are cited with both the GitHub URL and the local path.

## KQ1. Disney Research BD-X: "Design and Control of a Bipedal Robotic Character" (RSS 2024) and "Interactive Design of Stylized Walking Gaits for Robotic Characters" (SIGGRAPH/TOG 2024)

### Takeaway
Disney's pipeline is "animator authors kinematic references (Maya clips + a procedural, model-based gait tool); one RL policy per motion type tracks them with an imitation reward; a runtime animation engine blends layers (background loop, triggered clips, joystick offsets) into *command signals*; the operator switches policies by button." Motion matching is **not** used; balance comes from the RL policy departing from the reference under disturbance, and the whole thing was show-proven (about 10 h of public runtime without a fall) but is non-autonomous and costs one policy per episodic motion.

### Cited Findings

**Hardware and rates (RSS 2024) [HW]**
- Legs: 5 DoF each (hip yaw, hip roll, hip pitch, knee pitch, ankle pitch), plus a 4-DoF neck/head; ankle roll is passive; rounded urethane-foam soles. Mass 15.4 kg, height 0.66 m — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Actuators are quasi-direct-drive with 34 N·m (hip ab/ad, hip flex, knee) and 24 N·m (hip rotation, ankle, lower neck) peak torque; three high-gear-ratio head actuators (4.8 N·m). Actuator model tables list Unitree A1, Go1 and Dynamixel XH540-V150 parameters — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Policy runs at **50 Hz**; a microcontroller board talks to actuators and IMU at 600 Hz; first-order hold between policy updates followed by a low-pass filter with 37.5 Hz cutoff; redundant WiFi + LoRa to the operator — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Actuator model in training: τ = kP(a − q̃) − kD·q̇ with kP/kD = 15.0/0.6 (A1 model), 10.0/0.3 (Go1), 5.0/0.2 (XH540); friction, velocity-dependent torque limits, backlash and noise are modelled — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

**Authoring pipeline: what is authored vs learned**
- Episodic motions (happy dance, excited, jump, tantrum) are authored in Maya and used directly as references, not retargeted. Walking cycles come from a procedural gait tool built on rigid-body dynamics: artists supply reference gaits at several speeds as task-space torso and end-effector trajectories, and an MPC (Wieber-style) plans CoM/CoP while an inverse-dynamics controller (Koolen-style) turns this into whole-body trajectories; artists preview and adjust in an interactive tool — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Perpetual standing references are produced by inverse dynamics that satisfies the commands while optimising the remaining DoF so the CoP sits at the middle of the support polygon — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Motions are stored in a "path frame": while standing it converges to the feet centre; while walking it integrates the commanded path velocity, projected to a maximum distance from the torso — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Authored: kinematic references, reward weights and command ranges, animation clips, joystick mapping, the walking gait library. Learned: closed-loop joint-setpoint policies that track the references, including recovery from pushes/obstacles and the policy-specific response to commands — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

**Runtime animation engine (the "blend" layer)**
- Three layers: (1) background layer, a looped periodic animation with blinking/antenna motion, always active; (2) triggered layer, operator-selected clips blended in with weights β (show functions, ramp 0.1 s) and α (body configuration, ramp 0.35 s), linear blending for positions/joint angles and slerp for body orientation; (3) joystick layer, a nonlinear mapping that adds offsets to gaze and posture while standing or produces velocity commands while walking. Show functions are modulated by state (antennas duck and eye radii narrow at high walking speed) — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- **No motion matching.** Selection is manual via buttons plus policy switching. Transitions into walking are phase-aware (start phase follows turn direction); transitions out of walking wait for the next double-support phase — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Clips are mapped to remote buttons (yes/no, scan; happy, angry, anxious, curious); the number of buttons limits what a puppeteer can control — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

**RL policy design**
- Observation: torso horizontal position and yaw in path frame, torso linear/angular velocity in body frame, joint positions/velocities, previous two actions; conditioning inputs are a phase signal and a command vector. Walking phase features: sin/cos of 2πkφ, k ∈ {1,2}; episodic phase: 50 Gaussian basis functions over phase. Critic gets noise-free inputs plus randomised friction as privileged info — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Action: joint position setpoints for PD control, mapped so 0 = nominal pose and 1 = expected range per joint; setpoints clipped to a maximum deviation from measured position chosen so maximum torque can still be produced — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Commands: standing = head height/orientation offsets + torso height/orientation in path frame; walking = head offsets + 2D path velocity + turning rate; episodic = no command, phase drives the motion — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Reward weights (Table I): imitation of torso xy position 1.0, torso orientation 1.0, lin vel xy 1.0 and z 1.0, ang vel xy 0.5 and z 0.5, **leg joint positions 15.0, neck joint positions 100.0**, leg joint velocities 1e-3, neck joint velocities 1.0, contact match 1.0; regularisation: torques 1e-3, joint accelerations 2.5e-6, leg action rate 1.5, neck action rate 5.0, leg action acceleration 0.45, neck action acceleration 5.0; a survival term (value 20.0 appears, table partly garbled); early termination on head/torso ground contact or head–torso self-collision — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Domain randomisation: actuator parameters within measured ranges, encoder offset U(−0.02, 0.02) rad per episode, backlash U(0.005, 0.015) rad (A1), reflected inertia ±20%, velocity-dependent position noise, observation noise, mass and friction, random disturbance forces/torques on torso, head, hips and feet (short/small 0–5 N; short/large pelvis 90–150 N) ramped in over the first 1500 iterations; terrain randomised for walking only — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Training: Isaac Gym with custom actuator models, PPO, 8192 envs × 24 steps, 3×512 ELU MLP, 100k iterations (about 22 days on an RTX 4090), nominal behaviour by about 1500 iterations (~30 min). **Policies are trained separately, one per motion or motion type** — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

**Balance vs style**
- Under disturbances the policy departs from the reference trajectory and contact schedule to recover, which the authors present as an advantage over optimisation approaches constrained to the contact reference — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Ablations: direct torso-velocity tracking produces rapid foot shuffling and clearance/slip penalties are hard to tune; phase + contact reference gives the right stepping but a stiff upright torso; feeding kinematic reference poses instead of phase converges to the same reward and visually identical motion (phase chosen so references need not be stored on the robot) — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- For the jump, contact weight had to be raised within a phase window, otherwise the policy "tends to cheat by keeping the toes on the ground"; knee actuators hit torque limits at push-off — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

**Operator interface / show controller**
- Two-joystick handheld (Steam Deck layout) showing battery, motor temperatures and link quality. Standing: left stick = posture (torso pitch/height, yaw/roll; head counter-rotates to hold gaze), right stick = gaze (head yaw/pitch, with additive torso motion when the neck saturates). Walking: left stick = longitudinal velocity + turn rate, L2/R2 lateral, R1 toggles walking and holding it raises velocity gain from 50% to 100%. Menu = motion stop (forces standing, freezes setpoints with high gains after 0.5 s) — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

**Results and stated limitations [HW]**
- Walking up to 0.7 m/s forward, 0.4 m/s lateral, 1.8 rad/s turn. Mean absolute joint tracking error: standing 0.035 rad, walking 0.123, excited 0.029, happy dance 0.027, jump 0.043, tantrum 0.032. Public shows with up to three robots, about 10 h of runtime "without a single fall" — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- Limitations: not autonomous (puppeteer can reduce believability), each episodic motion needs its own policy so training overhead grows with the motion count, button count limits control, obstacle walking and push recovery shown only qualitatively; future work explicitly includes "single-policy learning for multiple skills" and autonomy in the animation engine — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

**SIGGRAPH/TOG 2024 stylized gait tool (model-based stack, the "blend space")**
- Artist-directed system for authoring stylised bipedal walking gaits; artists use an interactive tool that produces motion in real time on a physical or simulated robot driven by a model-based control stack; each style is a set of sample parameters; example walk cycles are blended across velocities while keeping contact constraints; demonstrated on a custom free-walking robot plus two simulated bipeds with different DoF/proportions — [Disney Research page](https://la.disneyresearch.com/?p=27530); [TOG 2024, DOI 10.1145/3658227](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Interactive_Design_of_Stylized_Walking_Gaits_for_Robotic_Characters-5.pdf)
- The matching patent (US12293445, filed 2022, granted 2025) details the engine: a path integrator computes the path frame; a kinematic planner maps sample parameters to a footstep plan and task-space targets; an FSM switches double/single support by gait phase; a dynamic planner computes CoM and CoP trajectories via "a model-based optimization that attempts to stabilize the CoM while ensuring that the CoP satisfies the contact constraints"; an "optimization-based inverse dynamics solver" computes torques/accelerations/GRFs subject to friction cones and joint acceleration limits — [US12293445B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12293445B2/en)
- A style = **nine samples** at key velocities (forward fast/slow, step in place, reverse fast/slow, left/right strafe, left/right turn), each defined by time intervals, attributes (stance splay, width, X-bias) and function curves (sole translation/rotation in swing frame, pelvis Tz and rotations, arm/spine/neck joints). Pelvis horizontal translation is deliberately excluded so the dynamic planner can optimise it — [US12293445B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12293445B2/en)
- Blending is done in **parameter space on a gait-phase map** (contact events at 0, π/2, π, 3π/2, 2π) so contact timings always map to equivalent events; velocity blending is two-stage (forward/reverse by x with cubic smoothing f(α) = −2α³ + 3α², turn/strafe by polar angle) with a gain γ ∈ (0,1); the gait phase integrates the blended phase rate, not wall-clock time — [US12293445B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12293445B2/en)
- Disturbance handling: if the whole-body controller cannot track the desired CoM, the dynamic planner adjusts the desired gait velocity via a feedback signal, "adjusting the upcoming footholds in the direction of a disturbance". The editor warns when double support falls below a minimum; the simulator "strictly enforces the dynamic constraints" while the engine "can violate the dynamic constraints to prevent the character from falling" during early design — [US12293445B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12293445B2/en)

**Disney follow-up, AMOR (SIGGRAPH 2025) [HW]**
- One tracking policy conditioned on a vector of reward weights (multi-objective PPO); weights are sampled from a simplex per episode and can be tuned **after training**. Objectives: joint-position tracking for upper body, lower body and feet, rigid-body pose, root orientation, velocity, and smoothness (torques + action rate) — [arXiv 2505.23708](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23708v1)
- Deployed on a 20-DoF bipedal robot at **50 Hz**; "tuning the reward weights on the real system enables the robot to perform physically demanding motions" (stationary dance where more smoothness weight reduced jitter and the sim-to-real gap; a double pirouette). Weight tuning took about 1 day vs 5 days to train the policy. A high-level policy that outputs weights online (trained on a discriminator reward) improved joint MAE 9.55° vs 10.02° but was shown only in simulation; "attempts to directly use the discriminator-based reward in the low-level policy led to mode collapse" — [arXiv 2505.23708](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23708v1)

### Inferences
- Disney's "governor" is a human with buttons plus an animation engine that produces *commands*, not joint angles; the policy is the only thing touching joints. The animation engine's layers are game-engine ideas (background loop, additive offsets from the joystick, cross-fade with ramp times), but they live entirely in command space.
- The two Disney systems divide the labour differently: the SIGGRAPH tool uses a classical model-based stack (footstep FSM → CoM/CoP optimiser → whole-body inverse dynamics) and blends *style parameters* across nine velocity samples; the RSS robot uses that tool only to produce references offline and leaves balance to RL. Both keep the pelvis horizontal position (the balance variable) out of the animator's hands.
- The explicit cost they report (one policy per episodic motion, 22 GPU-days per policy) is what AMOR and the "future work: single-policy learning for multiple skills" address, i.e. Disney itself moved toward one conditioned policy rather than more small ones.

### Gaps
- The SIGGRAPH paper PDF was too large to fetch; control rates, the exact MPC horizon and the physical robot's spec come only from the patent and the abstract page.
- No quantitative push-recovery or terrain numbers exist for the BD-X robot; the paper calls them qualitative.

## KQ2. Open Duck Mini v2 and other small hobby/research bipeds

### Takeaway
Open Duck Mini v2 is a near-literal miniature of Disney's recipe: a placo-based parametric walk engine generates reference gaits over a (vx, vy, wz) command grid, fitted to polynomials in phase; a single MJX/PPO joystick policy at 50 Hz tracks them with Disney's imitation reward; head motion is learned as part of the policy and then *additively offset* by the operator's head command at runtime, with lower servo P gains on the head. ToddlerBot, Berkeley Humanoid Lite and Zeroth are the same shape (one RL joystick walk + button-launched other behaviours, no learned transitions).

### Cited Findings

**Open Duck Mini v2 hardware and lineage [HW]**
- About 42 cm tall, BOM under $400, Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W as onboard computer, policies run by the separate Open_Duck_Mini_Runtime repo; "We are switching to Mujoco Playground"; reference motion generation for imitation learning in a separate repo; actuator identification with Rhoban's BAM — [Open_Duck_Mini README](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Mini) (local copy `official_repo/README.md`)
- The sim2real doc: "we are using cheap servomotors that are hard to model and not overly powerful"; BAM-identified Feetech STS3215 7.4 V parameters are exported to MuJoCo units (damping, kp, frictionloss, armature, forcerange); "We obtained good results by implementing the imitation reward described by Disney in their BDX paper"; reference motion comes from "a parametric walk engine" — [Open_Duck_Mini docs/sim2real.md](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Mini/blob/main/docs/sim2real.md) (local `official_repo/docs/sim2real.md`)

### Inferences
- On the duck as shipped, the "foundation" and the "walk control" are the same network: one joystick policy owns standing (zero command), walking, turning and head pose. There is no separate balance layer; disturbance robustness comes from the push randomisation and the IMU/backlash/delay noise model.
- Head expression is layered additively *after* the policy and softened by a 4× lower servo gain; this is the one place the current duck already does game-style additive layering, and it works because the head is light relative to the trunk.
- Every small servo biped surveyed (duck, ToddlerBot, Humanoid Lite, Z-Bot, Sigmaban) runs the learned or model-based walk at 25–50 Hz and treats the other behaviours as button-launched scripts or keyframes with no learned transition; nobody at this scale has published a learned governor.

### Gaps
- The `auto_gait.json` sweep ranges and the exact placo walk parameters used for v2 are not in the local tree and the GitHub README does not list them.
- No quantitative hardware metrics (fall rate, velocity tracking, push magnitude) are published for Open Duck Mini v2; the only hardware evidence is videos and the Hackaday summary.
- Mini-BDX v1's AWD (Isaac Gym, adversarial) results are only referenced, not documented; I found no write-up of why the project moved from AWD to the imitation reward.

## KQ3. Gait libraries and blend spaces in robotics

### Takeaway
Blend spaces have real hardware pedigrees: Cassie interpolated an HZD gait library by measured sagittal velocity (7 gaits, later 1331 indexed by vx, vy, height); Disney blends nine style samples in phase-aligned parameter space; Walk These Ways turned the blend space into an 8-D behaviour vector that one RL policy is conditioned on; Boston Dynamics' Choreographer composes authored moves as *requests* to a balance controller that has priority. The consistent lesson is that the interpolation variable must be something the controller can hold (velocity, phase) and that balance must be able to override the blend.

### Cited Findings

**Cassie HZD gait library (Michigan) [HW]**
- Seven gaits optimised for average sagittal velocity from −0.5 to +1.0 m/s in 0.25 m/s steps, all with lateral velocity 0 and step time 0.4 s; linear interpolation where "the interpolation parameter is the robot's filtered sagittal velocity" (measured, not commanded); lateral velocity handled by a leg-angle regulator, not the library — [Gong et al. 2019, arXiv 1809.07279](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.07279)
- Layers: nine virtual-constraint outputs (torso roll/pitch, stance hip yaw, stance/swing leg length, swing leg roll/pitch, swing hip yaw, swing foot pitch; stance foot pitch passive with zero torque) tracked by joint-level PD with 18 tuned gains; a four-parameter foot-placement regulator for longitudinal/lateral speed (without it the closed loop is unstable, with (ẋ, ẏ) acting as approximate integrators); a separate standing controller; implemented in real-time Simulink — [arXiv 1809.07279](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.07279)
- Walked on sidewalks, grass, slopes, waxed floors, snow, sand, through a controlled burn; a fall came from an operator button error that sped the robot up; sand made the narrow feet sink; a vendor software update broke the controller and a hip upgrade required retuning; amplifiers, motor dynamics and network delays were unmodelled so virtual constraints were only approximately enforced — [arXiv 1809.07279](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.07279)
- A later version used 1331 HZD gaits indexed by sagittal velocity, lateral velocity and walking height, blending neighbouring gaits online — [arXiv 2203.02570](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02570)
- The feedback-control report describes the library as letting the full model be "gain-scheduled by the current velocity", producing a continuum of gaits — [NSF PAR 10096925](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10096925)

**NaviGait (2025, BRUCE small humanoid)**
- "leverages a library of offline-optimized gaits and smoothly interpolates between them to produce continuous reference motions in response to high-level commands"; the RL policy "provides both joint-level and velocity command residual corrections to modulate and stabilize the reference trajectories"; claims faster training than conventional/imitation RL and the closest adherence to the reference; hardware shown on BRUCE in the video — [NaviGait project page](https://dynamicmobility.github.io/navigait/); [arXiv 2510.11542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11542v2) [HW, qualitative]

**Walk These Ways (CoRL 2022, Go1) [HW]**
- One policy conditioned on an 8-D behaviour vector b_t = phase offsets (θ1, θ2, θ3), stepping frequency f, body height, body pitch, stance width, footswing height, plus a 3-D velocity command. Gait presets: pronk (0,0,0), trot (0.5,0,0), bound (0,0.5,0), pace (0,0,0.5), gallop (0.25,0,0) — [ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
- 50 Hz on a Jetson TX2 NX, PD kp 20 / kd 0.5, 30-step observation history, MLP [512, 256, 128], ~20 ms latency modelled as constant action delay; the gait enters as clock inputs sin(2πt) per foot with desired contact states from a smoothed von Mises CDF — [ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
- "all gait parameters are consistently tracked after sim-to-real transfer"; stairs, slippery/granular terrain, crawling under a 22 cm bar, a 90 bpm dance. Costs: the gait-free baseline tracks velocity better in-distribution (yaw reward 0.76 vs 0.70), "our behavior parameterization is restrictive for combinations of high linear and angular velocity", no terrain randomisation, and a human must tune behaviour manually — [ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
- Rationale: the task is under-specified, "there are multiple equally good solutions" that generalise differently; auxiliary rewards act as biases for generalisation — [ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)

**Boston Dynamics Choreographer (Spot) [HW]**
- Moves are "holistically optimized requests to the controller, rather than pre-scripted motion", "prioritizing balance first and then following the specified steps"; "Spot will find the possible motion most similar to what was requested and do that instead"; animation from Maya "is still treated as a request to the balancing controller"; "the layering framework enabled us to combine the animated arm motion with the stock moves" — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/from-artistic-vision-to-hardware-how-to-make-spot-dance/)
- Tracks: Legs, Body, Arm, Gripper, Lights, plus Annotations; "All dance moves control one or more tracks" and "Moves that control any of the same tracks cannot run simultaneously"; timing in slices (4 per beat); "the robot cannot perform some combinations of moves, parameters, and other settings under all conditions"; entrance state (sit/stand) and a start delay are needed for the transition; Stop freezes with four feet down; "Choreography moves can induce unpredictable or unstable motions in Spot" — [BD developer docs](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/choreography/choreographer)
- Interrupting a dance mid-motion or starting another before it finishes "may cause falls" — [BD blog via search summary](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/in-step-with-spot/)

**Agility Robotics Digit**
- Agility moved locomotion to RL in Isaac Lab; early policies "would walk confidently and robustly" in simulation "but in the real world, it would slip and slide around wildly" because of simplified collision geometry, actuator energy-propagation inaccuracies and "instabilities in how constraints are solved in our unique closed-chain kinematics"; they spent six months on root causes rather than patch rewards; stacked rewards "pile up, and they cloud the original objective" and a policy "behaves unpredictably when composed with new rewards", so the improved stack uses "a handful of rewards around things like energy consumption and symmetry" — [Agility blog](https://agilityrobotics.com/content/crossing-sim2real-gap-with-isaaclab) [HW]
- An earlier Ohio State cascade (learned high-level policy producing reference trajectories, model-based low-level tracker) was reported as the first learned policy on Digit hardware — [arXiv 2103.15309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15309v1) [HW]

### Inferences
- Across Cassie, Disney and Walk These Ways the "blend space" axis is velocity (plus phase), and a separate regulator (foot placement, dynamic planner, RL) is always needed to make the interpolated gait stable; the library alone is never closed-loop stable.
- Choreographer is the clearest precedent for "authored move on top of a balance controller that wins": it is also the clearest statement that interrupting or overlapping moves on the same body track is where falls happen.
- For the duck, the equivalent of Cassie's 7-gait library already exists (placo sweep → polynomials in (vx, vy, wz, phase)); Walk These Ways shows that exposing style parameters (step frequency, foot height, stance width, body height/pitch) as policy inputs is tractable at 50 Hz with an MLP and gives runtime style control without retraining.

### Gaps
- No public technical description of Agility's "behaviour library" or of Unitree/ANYbotics skill libraries was found; vendor material is promotional.
- NaviGait's control rate, residual magnitudes and hardware numbers were not retrievable (PDF only).

## KQ4. Multi-skill composition and switching: governors, blending, distillation, and the "one tracking policy vs many" question

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 humanoid literature has converged on **one tracking policy per motion library** (GMT, BeyondMimic, TWIST, KungfuBot2, HOVER, LooperMuscle), with per-motion specialists (Disney RSS 2024, KungfuBot/PBHC) kept only for maximum dynamism. Switching between separately trained policies is the documented failure point: a plain tracking policy succeeds 2% of the time on cross-skill transitions in Switch's benchmark, and BeyondMimic's robot "tends to stumble at the start and end of motions". Working governors are therefore either (a) a scheduler that selects *which reference* a single tracker follows, with explicit similarity thresholds and an e-stop, or (b) a learned gate that blends expert *outputs* (MELA, GMT's MoE teacher), not a hand-written FSM that hot-swaps whole policies.

### Cited Findings

**One policy for a motion library**
- GMT (2025, Unitree G1, 23 DoF): a single unified policy trained on 8,925 curated clips (33.12 h) from AMASS and LAFAN1; 500 Hz sim / **50 Hz control**; MoE in the *teacher* only (gating outputs a distribution over experts, action = weighted sum), then DAgger distillation to a student with a 20-step proprioception history and ~2 s of future reference compressed to a 128-D latent; adaptive sampling by per-motion completion level; ablations: full 42.07 mm MPKPE vs 42.53 without MoE and 43.54 without adaptive sampling (AMASS); without adaptive sampling the policy failed to learn a 240 s composite and "struggled to balance", making deployment impossible; no get-up or rolling, no terrain inputs — [arXiv 2506.14770](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14770v2) [HW, qualitative]
- BeyondMimic (2025): "each motion is trained with its own policy but under a shared formulation and training setup", yet many clips were "trained jointly with multiple other skills" (some references > 3 min; ~2.5 h of motion; "hundreds of skills"); observation has no temporal stacking (motion phase, 9-D anchor pose error, 6-D IMU twist, joint pos/vel, last action); actions are joint setpoints "intentionally not clipped by joint kinematic limits"; low-impedance PD preferred on hardware because high gains "amplify sensor noise"; no system ID but armature must be accurate (zero armature caused overswinging and self-collisions); 30 clips (15 min) deployed; injected delay of 2 ms raised error, 5 ms caused one failure, 10 ms failed two of three trials — [arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4) [HW]
- BeyondMimic composition: tracking policies are distilled into a VAE latent, then a state–latent diffusion model (0.64 s horizon) is steered at test time by summed classifier-guidance costs (waypoint + SDF obstacle avoidance), keyframe inpainting at 0.2 s intervals drives transitions into cartwheels/spin-kicks, and walk→run transitions emerge from velocity commands although "rare and unlabeled in the motion data"; sim-to-sim cartwheel success 5% without the latent encoding vs 95% with it; failure modes: "the robot is stable once a gait orbit is established, but tends to stumble at the start and end of motions", larger guidance weights "can destabilize denoising during mode switching", history can trap the model in repetitive gaits — [arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4)
- HOVER (ICRA 2025, Unitree H1, 19 DoF): one policy with a mode mask (per upper/lower body: kinematic position, joint angle, or root tracking) and a sparsity mask, bits ~ Bernoulli(0.5) resampled per episode; DAgger from an oracle; 25-step proprioception stack; in simulation the multi-mode policy beats each specialist on at least 7 of 12 metrics per mode (e.g. ExBody-mode global MPJPE 185 vs 275 mm) and beats a from-scratch multi-mode RL baseline on "32/32 metrics and modes"; real-world on 20 standing motions it beats specialists on 11 of 12 metrics (48.9 vs 51.3 mm); runtime mode switching (ExBody→H2O while walking) shown only qualitatively; "Future work will explore further developing an automated mode-switching module" — [arXiv 2410.21229](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21229v2) [HW]
- ExBody (2024, H1): upper body imitates mocap, legs only track velocity, because including lower-body features "tends to over-constrain the problem" and yields brittle policies; 780 CMU clips (3.7 h); 11 real-world motions; without reference state initialisation mean episode length collapsed to 0.23; full-body tracking produced extra stepping; AMP produced straight-leg stumbling gaits — [ar5iv 2402.16796](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.16796) [HW]
- HumanPlus (2024): a 33-DoF humanoid; low-level policy trained in simulation on "existing 40-hour human motion datasets", used for real-time shadowing from an RGB camera, then behaviour cloning of skills with "60-100% success" from "up to 40 demonstrations" — [arXiv 2406.10454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10454) [HW]
- TWIST (CoRL 2025): "a single unified neural network controller" for manipulation, locomotion and expressive movement, trained with RL + behaviour cloning, deployed on Unitree G1 and Booster T1 — [TWIST project page](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/TWIST/index.html) [HW]
- KungfuBot / PBHC (NeurIPS 2025): each policy "trained for a single motion", maximising dynamism but with no multi-motion generalisation — [arXiv 2506.12851](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12851v2); KungfuBot2 / VMS (2025) answers with "a unified whole-body controller" using an Orthogonal Mixture-of-Experts and a segment-level tracking reward, "stable performance over minute-long sequences" — [arXiv 2509.16638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16638) [HW]
- LooperMuscle (Aug 2026, G1, 29 DoF): a structured MoE actor with per-joint-group gating over G = 2 semantic groups (upper/lower body), lower temperature for "sharper selection (for stability-critical lower-body)" and softer blending for the expressive upper body; converges in ~45 min vs ~6 h for PPO with 47.5% higher reward than FastSAC; deployed on G1 running a KungfuBot2 motion sequence — [arXiv 2608.00820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00820) (local `my_ax_2608.00820.txt`) [HW]

**Switching and transitions: what breaks**
- Switch (Apr 2026, G1): a Skill Graph whose nodes are reference frames, edges are consecutive frames plus cross-skill nearest neighbours under an L1 distance over joint positions/velocities and body positions (yaw and xy removed); one tracking policy trained with transition-biased RSI, buffer nodes and a foot–ground contact reward; an online scheduler fires on init, a user target change, end of reference, or a similarity crossing thresholds A/B: if sim ≤ A attach directly, if sim ≥ B **emergency stop**, otherwise score top-k candidates by λ·cost + V*. Sim-to-sim (MuJoCo, 50 trials/level): Switch 100% success at all difficulties vs GMT 30/10/2%; a base policy trained on single-skill data only scores **2.00%** at every level; base + skill graph alone reaches 100%; the NN planner is used on hardware for latency; a 500 N disturbance during a kick is recovered only with the scheduler — [arXiv 2604.14834](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14834v1) [HW, qualitative]
- Light-Loco-Parkour (Aug 2026): one deployable policy from depth + velocity command chooses among walking, balancing, climbing, stepping down, vaulting; skills are created from sparse seed motions expanded into terrain-paired references and "skill transitions from reward, with no state machine or runtime motion generator"; zero-shot to hardware indoors/outdoors — [arXiv 2608.02653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02653) [HW]. A search summary of the same paper describes a first stage that distils separately trained skills into one policy and a transition stage that learns when to invoke a skill vs keep walking, with a discriminator switched by phase — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02653)
- A 2026 loco-manipulation paper with a VLM router over expert policies notes that pure RL "may become physically inconsistent or unstable during skill switching and composition" — [arXiv 2603.08572](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08572v1)
- An expert-composer approach (2024, physics-based characters, sim-only) trains a separate composer policy to generate transitions between single-skill imitation experts — [arXiv 2403.11412](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11412v1) [SIM]
- Multiple adversarial motion priors (2022): several discrete styles/skills learned at once "without notable performance differences", demonstrated on a wheeled-legged robot — [arXiv 2203.14912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14912v1) [HW]

**Blending expert policies on hardware**
- MELA (Science Robotics 2020, quadruped): starts from "a distinct set of pre-trained experts, each in a separate deep neural network", a gating network learns to combine them, which yields "more specialised experts and transitional skills across various locomotion modes"; at runtime it "constantly blends multiple DNNs and dynamically synthesises a new DNN"; real quadruped "performed coherent trotting, steering, and fall recovery autonomously" — [arXiv 2012.05810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05810) [HW]
- Disney AMOR's two-stage design (frozen weight-conditioned low-level policy + high-level policy choosing weights) exists because putting the transition/style discriminator reward into the low-level policy "led to mode collapse" — [arXiv 2505.23708](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23708v1)

**Why not fine-tune one network per new skill**
- PPO "will prioritize the current situation at the expense of forgetting previously acquired skills" (quadruped terrain sequences) — [arXiv 2311.06828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06828v1.pdf) [SIM]; a continual-RL quadruped method (Piggyback-protected parameters + reinitialising unused ones) reports that traditional continual-learning methods performed poorly on locomotion — [Entropy 2024, PMC11154561](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11154561/) [SIM]
- Agility's engineering note that composed/stacked rewards make a policy behave unpredictably — [Agility blog](https://agilityrobotics.com/content/crossing-sim2real-gap-with-isaaclab)

**Older foundations**
- DeepMimic (2018): RL tracking of reference clips with reference state initialisation and early termination — [arXiv 1804.02717](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02717) [SIM]; AMP (2021): a discriminator style reward replaces hand-written imitation terms — [arXiv 2104.02180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02180) [SIM]; AMP on a Unitree A1 from 4.5 s of German Shepherd mocap gave energy-efficient gaits with natural transitions — [arXiv 2203.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15103), [code](https://github.imc.re/Alescontrela/AMP_for_hardware) [HW]

### Inferences
- The field's answer to "many small policies + governor vs one tracker" is unambiguous at humanoid scale: one tracker, and the governor selects references, not networks. The strongest direct evidence is Switch's 2% vs 100% (same policy class, only difference is whether transitions were in the training distribution) and HOVER's multi-mode-beats-specialists tables.
- The reason is state-distribution mismatch at the handoff: a policy only knows the states its own reference produced. Every working system either trains transitions explicitly (Switch, Light-Loco-Parkour, ToddlerBot's end-pose RSI), or distils everything into one network (HOVER, GMT, BeyondMimic VAE), or blends outputs through a learned gate (MELA).
- "Policy switching with an FSM" is what Disney shipped, but note the guard rails: switches happen only at double support, with a motion-stop fallback and a policy that was trained with large random pushes so it can absorb the switch transient.
- Where governors do exist (Switch), they are small (a similarity test + graph search + e-stop) and run on top of one tracker; this is the shape most likely to transfer to a Pi Zero 2 W.

### Gaps
- No paper quantifies action-space cross-fading between two independently trained RL policies on hardware; the only hardware blending result (MELA) blends inside one jointly trained system.
- Switch's thresholds A/B, graph size and control rate are unreported; its hardware results are qualitative.
- No small (sub-5 kg, servo) biped has published a learned multi-skill tracker or a learned skill scheduler.

## KQ5. Classical layering that works on position-controlled servos, and what is realistic at 50 Hz on a Pi Zero 2 W with P-only servos

### Takeaway
On P-only hobby servos the working classical stack is: open-loop CPG/spline gait → IMU-driven corrective offsets (hip, arm, foot angle, CoM shift, timing) → footstep adjustment from a fitted LIP model (capture steps), all at 100 Hz on a NUC-class CPU with ~0.1 ms per iteration; the RL-on-reference approach (Peng 2020, Disney, Open Duck) replaces the middle two layers with a network while keeping position targets as the action. Residual RL on top of a model-based base is attractive but its best recent results (Yan 2026) are simulation-only and torque-based.

### Cited Findings

**IMU-feedback layers on P-controlled servos (NimbRo / igus)**
- igus Humanoid Open Platform: servos use "pure proportional position control", torque τ = K_c·V_B·K_p(q_d − q); feed-forward compensation optional; sensors are joint positions and a 6-axis IMU; the gait is a Missura-style CPG with phase μ[n+1] = μ[n] + π·f_g·Δt built in abstract → inverse → joint space — [Allgeuer & Behnke, arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712) [HW]
- Feedback mechanisms driven by fused pitch/roll deviations: arm angle (P, D, some I), hip angle (P, D, some I), support foot angle (P, D, phase-gated), continuous foot angle (I), CoM shifting (I, primary), virtual slope (pitch, deadband, scaled by sagittal command), timing (roll, adjusts gait frequency), plus capture-step step sizes; corrective action u = K·e with a 5×6 gain matrix of 10–14 nonzero entries; PD gains tuned first, then LQR on a fitted second-order sagittal model (poles 236.3 and 16.77 rad/s), usually needing manual retuning — [arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712)
- Results: walked onto an unexpected 1.5 cm step with P/D feedback (fell without); integral feedback rejected pitch error "in about 5 s"; timing feedback returned fused roll to its limit cycle "within 2.5 s" after a lateral push that caused a fall without it; 21 cm/s on artificial grass; at RoboCup 2016 "none of the five robots fell while walking" (excluding collisions) — [arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712)
- Capture Steps (IJHR 2020, NimbRo-OP2: 134.5 cm, 17.5 kg, Dynamixel MX-106R in low-gain compliant position mode with "imprecise position tracking", encoders + torso IMU, no F/T or foot contact sensing, parallel-link legs without ankle pitch): **100 Hz, 0.12 ms per iteration on a 1.3 GHz core**; state = forward kinematics rotated so trunk attitude matches IMU roll/pitch, CoM taken as the point between the hips in the support-foot frame, velocities by numerical differentiation (noisy), predictive filter with 0.054 s latency compensation; LIP constant C identified experimentally; step time and footstep from LIP orbital energy and end-of-step prediction; "Balance is corrected only by modifying step size and timing" (ZMP is computed but not sent); pushes by hand: open loop 22 falls / 58 pushes, capture steps 18 falls / 97 pushes; support-foot detection "does not scale to non-planar surfaces" — [arXiv 2011.02793](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.02793v1) [HW]

**Capture-point feedback on a position-controlled kid-size humanoid**
- SURENA-Mini (53 cm, 23 DoF, Dynamixel, IMU + encoders only, 100 Hz on an embedded PC): ξ = x_c + ẋ_c/ω_n; ankle, hip and arm target angles all set by the same PD law on capture-point error (θ = k_p·ξ_err + k_d·ξ̇_err); gains tuned by trial and error, values unreported; ankle strategy limited to CoP inside the support polygon, hip strategy needed when CP leaves it; the terms act together with no priority; stepping not implemented; with the controller the robot recovered from ball-impact pushes it otherwise fell to, no magnitudes reported — [arXiv 1710.10598](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.10598) [HW]

**Model-based walk on Dynamixel position servos (Rhoban 2023)**
- Preview-control CoM QP every 25 ms (2 ms solve), whole-body IK QP at 200 Hz (100 µs), footsteps from a TD3 policy (100 µs), feedback only from foot pressure; trajectories "were not planned around actuator limits" so knee/ankle hit velocity limits; MPC planned for larger instabilities — [arXiv 2402.00612](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00612) [HW]

**CPG + RL**
- CPG-RL (RA-L 2022, Unitree A1): RL modulates oscillator parameters; sim-to-real with added mass up to 115% of nominal and a 13.75 kg dynamically added load; deployable "with no domain randomization and very little feedback", including policies observing only contact booleans — [arXiv 2211.00458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00458) [HW]; Visual CPG-RL adds terrain perception on Go1 — [arXiv 2212.14400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14400v2) [HW]
- Earlier biped CPG learning (Matsubara 2006, Endo 2008) was policy-gradient tuning of CPG feedback on a 5-link biped / humanoid hardware, improving "within 200 iterations" — [search summary](https://bicr.atr.jp//~xmorimo/PDF/Matsubara2006RAS.pdf)

**Reference motion + RL with position targets (the Peng 2020 recipe)**
- Laikago, policy at **30 Hz**, actions are PD targets passed through a Butterworth low-pass; observation = three previous poses and actions (IMU roll/pitch/yaw + joint rotations, no root position) plus target poses at t+1, t+2, t+10, t+30 (~1 s look-ahead) and a dynamics latent; reward 0.5 pose + 0.05 joint vel + 0.2 end-effector + 0.15 root pose + 0.1 root velocity with exponential shaping; retargeting by IK on paired foot/hip keypoints; DR on mass ×[0.8,1.2], inertia, motor strength, friction, latency [0, 0.04] s — [ar5iv 2004.00784](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.00784) [HW]
- Hardware returns: adaptive policies best on most skills (dog pace 0.827, spin 0.751); non-randomised policies "fail to transfer"; "Running Man" failed because the policy learned to "keep the robot's feet on the ground and shuffle backwards"; "The behaviors learned by our policies are currently not as stable as the best manually-designed controllers"; could not learn large jumps/runs — [ar5iv 2004.00784](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.00784)

**Residual RL on a model-based base**
- Yan et al. (Jan 2026): DCM/VRP planner + inverse-dynamics whole-body controller as base, residual *torque* policy (2-layer LSTM) at 200 Hz, oracle-supervised loss (ω_sup = 10); 100% success on Kangaroo, BRUCE and H1-2 in MuJoCo vs 0% for the base policy under randomisation and 10/0/0% for unsupervised residual RL; the oracle assumes flat ground and "cannot anticipate abrupt command changes"; **no hardware experiments** — [arXiv 2601.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16109v1) [SIM]
- A modular residual framework adds an RL residual to a heuristic footstep planner, then supervised residuals elsewhere — [arXiv 2507.18138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18138); kernel-based residual learning pairs a frozen MPC-imitating kernel with an RL residual for balance recovery on a quadruped — [arXiv 2302.07343](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.07343); NaviGait adds joint-level and velocity-command residuals to an interpolated HZD gait library on BRUCE — [NaviGait](https://dynamicmobility.github.io/navigait/) [HW, qualitative]

**Position-mode RL policies on hardware and their rates**
- Disney BD-X 50 Hz with 600 Hz actuator link and a 37.5 Hz low-pass on the hold — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1); Walk These Ways 50 Hz, 30-step history, [512,256,128] MLP on a Jetson TX2 NX — [ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238); ToddlerBot 50 Hz on a Jetson Orin NX CPU — [arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v4); Berkeley Humanoid Lite 25 Hz — [arXiv 2504.17249](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17249v1); GMT 50 Hz — [arXiv 2506.14770](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14770v2); Open Duck Mini v2 50 Hz on a Pi Zero 2 W with servo kp 30 and an "exceeded budget" warning in the loop — local `sim/upstream_runtime/scripts/v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py`
- BeyondMimic: low-impedance PD preferred on hardware because high gains "amplify sensor noise" and reduce passive compliance — [arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4); Disney: setpoints clipped to a maximum deviation from measured position so max torque is still producible — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

### Inferences
- With P-only servos the robot's "foundation layer" is physically a stiffness controller whose only tunable per-joint quantity is kp; every surveyed servo biped therefore keeps the policy or the walk engine in *position-target* space and uses either (a) low kp with the policy absorbing compliance (BeyondMimic, NimbRo low-gain mode), or (b) a setpoint-clip/low-pass to protect the servos (Disney, Peng 2020, Open Duck's optional filter).
- A classical balance layer that needs no F/T sensing exists and is cheap: Allgeuer's fused-angle offsets and Missura's capture steps together cost ~0.1 ms per 100 Hz tick on a 1.3 GHz core, and they were validated on 17.5 kg Dynamixel robots whose actuators are worse-tracking than the duck's relative to mass. The same arithmetic fits easily inside the duck's 20 ms budget alongside a small MLP.
- Residual-on-model-based is the least proven option at this scale: its best 2026 results are simulation-only, torque-controlled and 200 Hz, three things the duck does not have; the proven variants (NaviGait, CPG-RL) add residuals to a *kinematic* reference or oscillator, which is what the duck's polynomial reference already is.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark of P-only Feetech STS3215 position tracking bandwidth vs commanded step size; BAM parameters exist but no closed-loop bandwidth figure was found.
- No measured inference time for the duck's ONNX policy on the Pi Zero 2 W was found (the runtime only warns when the 20 ms budget is exceeded).

## KQ6. IK and terrain adaptation on robots with only an IMU and joint positions

### Takeaway
Blind terrain handling on hardware comes from three mechanisms: (1) the balance controller treating the authored motion as a request it may violate (Choreographer, Disney's RL policy departing from the contact schedule, the patent's foothold shift along the disturbance), (2) IMU-driven kinematic corrections (fused-angle foot/hip offsets, "virtual slope", late-touchdown clock delays), and (3) terrain randomisation in training; explicit "motion warping" of clips to measured terrain is not done on IMU-only robots because there is no terrain measurement to warp to.

### Cited Findings
- Choreographer: moves and imported Maya animations are requests to the balance controller; on a slippery floor "Spot will find the possible motion most similar to what was requested and do that instead" — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/from-artistic-vision-to-hardware-how-to-make-spot-dance/) [HW]
- Disney RSS 2024: terrain is randomised in training for walking only; the policy departs from reference and contact schedule under disturbance; obstacle walking shown qualitatively — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1) [HW]. Patent: the dynamic planner shifts "the upcoming footholds in the direction of a disturbance" when the whole-body controller cannot track the CoM — [US12293445B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12293445B2/en)
- Allgeuer: "virtual slope" (pitch deviation with deadband, scaled by sagittal velocity) kept the robot walking under continuous forward pushes; continuous foot-angle and CoM-shift integrators adapt to sustained tilt; walked onto an unexpected 1.5 cm step; "residual pitch limit cycles appear on uneven steps because the feet are not equally placed" — [arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712) [HW]
- Capture steps: rough terrain not tested; support-foot detection assumes a flat horizontal floor — [arXiv 2011.02793](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.02793v1)
- Rhoban: a late swing-foot touchdown (from foot pressure) delays the trajectory clock to match the real state — [arXiv 2402.00612](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00612) [HW]
- Cassie blind stairs: sim-to-real RL climbs and descends stairs without vision; adding stair-proximity inputs "partially recovered efficiency" — [arXiv 2105.08328](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2105.08328) [HW]. A 2026 paper argues stairs' repetitive structure "can often be handled using proprioception alone", while blind policies are "largely ineffective on terrain with pronounced height variations" — [search summary, arXiv 2603.07928](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.07928v1)
- GMT has no terrain observations and is "not designed for slopes or stairs" — [arXiv 2506.14770](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14770v2); BeyondMimic was tested outdoors on soft soil, leaves and uneven ground with a policy whose only exteroception-free inputs are IMU twist and joint state — [arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4) [HW]
- Open Duck Playground has `scene_mjx_rough_terrain.xml` / `rough_terrain_backlash` tasks alongside flat ones, and the runtime observation includes two foot-contact switches — [Open_Duck_Playground README](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Playground); local `v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py`
- Walk These Ways: trained on flat ground only, yet crossed stairs, curbs and granular terrain; most platform failures were in forward walking — [ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238) [HW]

### Inferences
- With IMU + joints + foot switches the only terrain signals are trunk tilt, tilt rate, and early/late touchdown; the robots that use them well (NimbRo, Rhoban) turn each into a specific kinematic offset or a clock adjustment rather than re-planning the clip.
- "Motion warping" in the game sense (bend the authored trajectory to hit a measured foothold) requires a foothold measurement the duck does not have; the realistic equivalent is the patent's/capture-step behaviour of shifting the *next* foothold by a balance variable (CoM/CP state), which is observable from IMU + kinematics.
- An RL tracker trained with rough-terrain and push randomisation is the cheapest way to get terrain tolerance on the duck, and every hardware example above that did so (Disney, BeyondMimic, Walk These Ways) got usable blind robustness; a kinematic-offset layer is the cheapest way to get it *without* retraining.

### Gaps
- No source measured how authored clip fidelity degrades under terrain on any of these robots; terrain results are pass/fail.
- No IMU-only foothold-height estimation method for sub-5 kg bipeds was found.

## KQ7. Push recovery and self-correction: triggers, strategies, and blending back

### Takeaway
The trigger variable in every working system is a divergent balance quantity (capture point / DCM, LIP orbital energy, or fused tilt + rate), not raw tilt alone; ankle/hip/arm strategies are continuous PD offsets on that error (SURENA-Mini), stepping is a discrete decision on predicted end-of-step state or a Lyapunov threshold (capture steps, KIST), and "blending back" is implicit: the corrective offsets decay with the error, or the next nominal step is planned from the corrected state. RL trackers trained with random pushes recover without any explicit trigger, but only within the push range they were trained on.

### Cited Findings

**Trigger features**
- Capture point ξ = x_c + ẋ_c/ω_n with ω_n = √(g/z_c): if ξ is inside the support polygon the robot recovers without hip strategy or a step; the capture region must intersect the swing-foot workspace for a one-step recovery — [arXiv 1710.10598](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.10598)
- Capture steps: no explicit CP; the LIP orbital energy and predicted end-of-step state select step time T (minimum time to reach the sagittal limit or lateral exchange point; 2 s fallback if orbital energy is positive) and lateral footstep S'y = λ√(ċ'y²/C² + α²) — [arXiv 2011.02793](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.02793v1)
- KIST Lyapunov approach: the reactive step is generated "only when the increase of the Lyapunov function during the current walking cycle reaches a threshold" — [search summary, KIST](https://pubs.kist.re.kr/handle/201004/113585)
- Khadiv et al.: nominal step location and duration are set at step start; each control cycle the landing point and time are updated so that "a desired offset between the swing foot landing point" and the end-of-step DCM is kept; "the swing foot trajectory is regenerated at each control cycle"; location-only adjustment can fail under severe pushes because the DCM still diverges — [arXiv 1610.02377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02377) [SIM]
- Allgeuer: fused pitch/roll deviation (and its filtered derivative, integral) drives every mechanism; timing feedback uses roll deviation to speed up or slow down the gait phase — [arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712)
- Switch's scheduler re-plans when similarity to the reference crosses threshold A and e-stops above threshold B; a 500 N push during a kick is recovered only with the scheduler active — [arXiv 2604.14834](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14834v1)

**Strategies on position-controlled robots [HW]**
- SURENA-Mini: ankle, hip and arm target angles from the same CP-error PD, summed with no priority; recovered from "severe" ball pushes it otherwise fell to; stepping not implemented — [arXiv 1710.10598](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.10598)
- Polaris (95 cm, Dynamixel): push recovery on inexpensive robots must work "with lower torque servos, less precise machining, and limited sensing and computation"; the hip strategy "is useful for small and medium disturbances, and is thus an improvement over COP" — [Modares journal](https://mme.modares.ac.ir/article_9709_416ebee8db53bd692a556c8b4c307bcc.pdf?lang=en)
- NimbRo-OP2 capture steps: 18 falls in 97 hand pushes vs 22 in 58 open-loop (authors caution push strengths differed) — [arXiv 2011.02793](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.02793v1)
- igus platform: lateral push at 3.2 s recovered "within 2.5 s" with timing feedback, same push at 8.2 s without it caused a fall — [arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712)
- Cassie: with low gains "a lean can cascade until the robot falls, so CoM y regulation is needed" — [arXiv 1809.07279](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.07279)
- ToddlerBot teleop balance: CoM controller + IMU torso-pitch PD — [arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v4)

**Learned recovery**
- Disney: random forces/torques on torso, head, hips and feet (0–5 N short/small, 90–150 N short/large on the pelvis) ramped in over 1500 iterations; the policy "departs from the reference trajectory and contact schedule to recover" — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1) [HW, qualitative]
- Open Duck Playground: pushes every 5–10 s, magnitude 0.1–1.0 (added to base xy velocity) — local `joystick.py`; Hackaday reports v2 "can remain impressively stable when shoved" — [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2025/04/05/disneys-bipedal-bdx-series-droid-gets-the-diy-treatment) [HW, anecdotal]
- MELA: fall recovery is one of the blended experts on a real quadruped — [arXiv 2012.05810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05810) [HW]
- GMT: get-up from a fall is explicitly unsupported — [arXiv 2506.14770](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14770v2); ExBody: "the occurrence of falls can sometimes result in broken parts" — [ar5iv 2402.16796](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.16796)
- Disney's "motion stop" freezes setpoints with high gains after 0.5 s and forces standing — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)

**Blending back into the nominal motion**
- Capture steps: the ZMP correction is never sent to the gait generator; only step size/timing are modified, so the next nominal step follows automatically — [arXiv 2011.02793](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.02793v1)
- Allgeuer: all corrections are additive offsets to the open-loop pose with soft (C¹) coercion limits; integral terms use an exponentially weighted integrator with a chosen half-life, which sets the decay back to nominal — [arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712)
- SURENA-Mini: not addressed; feedback simply drives CP error to the reference — [arXiv 1710.10598](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.10598)
- Choreographer: stop/interrupt mid-move is the fall risk; recovery from infeasible requests is "automatically try to correct the rest" inside the balance controller — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/from-artistic-vision-to-hardware-how-to-make-spot-dance/)

### Inferences
- For the duck the observable trigger set is: estimated CP/DCM from IMU-corrected forward kinematics (as capture steps does, CoM ≈ hip midpoint), fused tilt and tilt-rate deadbands, and early/late touchdown from the foot switches. All were used on servo robots with the same sensing.
- A practical recovery design has three tiers with increasing cost: additive IMU offsets (always on, decays by half-life), hip/arm strategy as a larger CP-error gain when CP leaves the foot, and a step modification when the predicted end-of-step state is unrecoverable; the animation resumes because the offsets are additive and the footstep is just the next nominal one.
- RL-learned recovery only covers what was randomised; a classical tier beneath it provides graceful degradation and a measurable "recover now" signal the governor can also use to pause or cancel clips (Switch's threshold-B e-stop is exactly that).

### Gaps
- No source gives tuned thresholds (CP margin, tilt deadband) for a 1.5–2 kg biped; all numeric tunings are for 3.6–17.5 kg robots.
- No hardware result on blending a learned recovery policy back into an authored clip; the closest is Switch's scheduler re-attaching to the graph.

## KQ8. Architectural recommendations for the duck (weighing the evidence)

### Takeaway
Keep **one** tracking/joystick policy as the foundation (it already owns stand, walk, turn, head, and push tolerance) and build the "library of controls" as a library of *references and commands* that this one policy tracks, with a small classical governor (phase-gated switching, similarity/CP thresholds, additive IMU corrections) rather than a library of separately trained networks that get hot-swapped.

### Cited Findings (the evidence weighed)
- Many-small-policies has a working show precedent (Disney RSS 2024) but with costs the authors themselves flag: one policy per episodic motion, switching only at double support, and "future work: single-policy learning for multiple skills" — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)
- One-tracker beats specialists in the only head-to-head tables available (HOVER sim and hardware; Switch 100% vs base 2%; GMT vs ExBody2) — [arXiv 2410.21229](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21229v2); [arXiv 2604.14834](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14834v1); [arXiv 2506.14770](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14770v2)
- The duck's own stack already tracks a (vx, vy, wz, phase)-indexed reference library with one 50 Hz policy and layers the head additively — local `joystick.py`, `custom_rewards.py`, `v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py`
- Style parameters as policy inputs are tractable at 50 Hz and give runtime control without retraining — [ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238); reward weights as policy inputs let style/balance be re-tuned on the real robot in a day — [arXiv 2505.23708](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23708v1)
- Classical IMU-offset + capture-step layers run in ~0.1 ms per 100 Hz tick on P-only Dynamixel robots and were validated on hardware — [arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712); [arXiv 2011.02793](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.02793v1)
- Interrupting or overlapping authored moves on the same body track is where falls happen — [BD docs](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/choreography/choreographer); start/end of motions is where a one-tracker + diffusion governor stumbles — [arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4)
- Fine-tuning a locomotion policy per new skill forgets old skills — [arXiv 2311.06828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06828v1.pdf); [PMC11154561](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11154561/)

### Inferences (recommendations)
- **Many small controls + governor vs one tracking policy.** Make the "controls" small *data*, not small *networks*: each control = a reference clip or a parametric gait sample (placo/Blender) + a command vector + metadata (entry/exit phase, allowed blend partners). One shared tracking policy (same reward template every time, as Disney and BeyondMimic do) tracks whatever the governor hands it. Replacing a control then means replacing a clip and, at most, re-running the one standard training job with the new clip added to the motion set, which is the "edit motions, not reward functions" principle with the evidence behind it. Separately trained per-control networks are only justified for a motion that the shared tracker demonstrably cannot learn (the KungfuBot/PBHC case), and such a network must be entered and exited only through a trained transition state (ToddlerBot's end-pose RSI, Switch's transition-biased RSI).
- **Where balance/self-correction should live.** In two places, on purpose: (1) inside the tracker, by training it with the duck's push/terrain/IMU-delay randomisation so it departs from the clip when shoved (this is what already works on the duck and on BD-X); (2) in a thin classical layer *between* governor and tracker that is always on and needs no retraining: IMU fused-tilt deadband offsets (hip/foot/CoM shift with half-life decay) added to the tracker's targets, a CP/orbital-energy estimate from IMU-rotated forward kinematics, and a gated "recover now" signal from CP-outside-foot or tilt-rate thresholds. The governor uses that signal to (a) freeze clip triggers, (b) widen the tracker's allowed deviation, and (c) above a second threshold drop to the stand/motion-stop control, as Disney's motion stop and Switch's threshold-B e-stop do. Do not implement recovery as a separate RL policy that is cross-faded in; no hardware evidence supports that, and MELA's blended fall-recovery expert was trained jointly with its gate.
- **The foundation layer given P-only servos.** Treat the STS3215 P loop as the lowest layer and model it as such (BAM kp/kv/forcerange/armature, as now); keep the tracker's action in position-target space with the duck's action scale and add the two cheap protections proven on hardware: a setpoint clip relative to measured position (Disney) and/or a low-pass on targets (Peng 2020, Disney 37.5 Hz hold filter, the runtime's optional filter), and a lower kp on the head than on the legs (already 8 vs 30). Add a model-based standing controller as a fallback foundation (CoP-centred stance from IK, ToddlerBot's CoM + IMU-pitch PD) so there is always something that stands without a network. Do not plan a torque-level or 200 Hz residual layer; the only strong results there are simulation-only and torque-controlled.
- **Governor design.** A phase-aware FSM/behaviour tree over controls with: switch-in only at the matching gait phase (Disney), switch-out of walking only at double support, Choreographer-style track ownership (legs vs head/antennas/eyes) so additive layers (head gaze, blinks, antenna mood) never contend with the leg track, ramped weights (0.1 s for show functions, ~0.35 s for body), a kinematic-similarity test before any transition (Switch's threshold A: attach directly only when the current pose is close to the target clip's entry frame), and the CP/tilt gate above. This is all O(µs) arithmetic and fits in the 20 ms budget next to an MLP.
- **Blend spaces.** Keep velocity + phase as the only blend axes for the leg track, pre-generate the samples (placo sweep), and blend in *parameter/phase space* (patent) rather than in joint space so contact timing is preserved; if style knobs are wanted (step height, cadence, stance width, trunk pitch), expose them to the tracker as Walk These Ways inputs during training rather than as post-hoc joint offsets, since the only hardware-validated runtime style control on an RL policy is input conditioning.
- **Terrain.** Rely on rough-terrain + push randomisation in the tracker plus the IMU-offset layer; do not attempt foothold warping (no sensing for it). Use foot switches for late-touchdown clock delay (Rhoban), which is the one terrain adaptation that works with the duck's sensors and needs no training.
- **Validation protocol implied by the evidence.** Measure what Disney and NimbRo did: falls per push session, mean absolute joint tracking error per control (BD-X: 0.03–0.12 rad), and transition success rate per (control A → control B) pair, since transitions, not controls, are where every surveyed system failed.

### Gaps
- No published hardware comparison of "one tracker + FSM governor" against "per-skill policies + FSM" on a servo biped; the recommendation extrapolates from humanoid-scale results and from the duck's current single-policy success.
- Compute headroom on the Pi Zero 2 W for a larger tracker (history stack, multi-clip conditioning) is unmeasured; the runtime only logs budget overruns.
