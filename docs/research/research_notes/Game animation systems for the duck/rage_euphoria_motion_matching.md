# RAGE/Euphoria, RDR2 locomotion, motion matching, and parkour/climbing animation systems (as of 2026-11)

Scope note: Rockstar publishes almost nothing technical. Everything below about GTA IV/V and RDR2 internals is either (a) a NaturalMotion/Rockstar interview quote, (b) a NaturalMotion product description from 2006-2012, or (c) community reverse engineering of the GTA V scripting API and RDR2 config files. Each finding says which. Motion matching and the Ubisoft/Naughty Dog/EA material is far better documented (GDC Vault abstracts, SIGGRAPH papers, Holden's blog).

Terminology used in the Inferences tables: **LIFE** = the mechanism's main contribution is character/liveliness/expressiveness; **ADAPT** = its main contribution is physical realism or adaptation to the environment/perturbation. **In/Out** = the runtime inputs and outputs of the mechanism as a black box.

## Q1. Euphoria: what is Dynamic Motion Synthesis, what are behaviours, how are they composed, how does it blend with animation, what happened after Zynga?

### Takeaway
Euphoria is NaturalMotion's runtime of "Dynamic Motion Synthesis" (DMS): a physically simulated articulated character driven by an "artificial nervous system" of tens of composable, parameterised behaviours (balance, catchFall, braceForImpact, pointGun, shot, grab, headLook, ...) that developers trigger and tune per situation; it was always a complement to keyframe/mocap animation and IK, not a replacement, and it is still referenced by Rockstar for RDR2 (2018) even though NaturalMotion stopped licensing it to anyone else in June 2017. The exact muscle/PD model is not documented publicly; what is public is the message/parameter interface reverse engineered from GTA V.

### Cited Findings

**What DMS/Euphoria is (vendor statements, 2006-2012)**
- Euphoria is NaturalMotion's run-time version of DMS, first introduced in the offline tool endorphin; "unlike traditional animation playback technology, euphoria is not based on canned data recall, but on fast simulations of the game character's actual motor control, muscles and biomechanics." — [Game Developer press release, 2006](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/naturalmotion-announces-euphoria-run-time-technology-for-next-generation-game-platforms)
- Torsten Reil began with computer simulations of human nervous systems as a DPhil student at Oxford's zoology/biology department to understand how they control movement; founded NaturalMotion in 2001 with Oxford University Innovation. — [Oxford Biology](https://www.biology.ox.ac.uk/node/2151801)
- Company founded November 2001 by Torsten Reil, Michael Buckland and Tariq Iqbal; Zynga acquired NaturalMotion in January 2014 for US$527M. — [Wikipedia: NaturalMotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NaturalMotion)
- Oxford University Innovation states a patent application on the technology was granted in 2012 (the patent text itself was not retrieved in this session). — [Oxford University Innovation](https://innovation.ox.ac.uk/?p=8954)
- Reil (2006): Euphoria's adaptive behaviours are "the artificial nervous system that replaces canned animations"; characters "know their body and muscles" and use sensors to read the environment; "they pretty much work like we do." It runs DMS at run time on the CPU, simulating the body and its nervous system; NaturalMotion staff "spend most of their time writing these adaptive behaviours for partner titles." Reil claimed it could "replace large libraries of mocap data." Early Indiana Jones demo characters looked "a bit floaty" when falling. — [PSU interview with NaturalMotion (2006)](https://www.psu.com/news/psu-interviews-naturalmotion/2/)
- Wikipedia's description: the engine animates characters "on the fly" from "full simulation of the 3D character, including body, muscles and motor nervous system," so actions/reactions vary between replays, going beyond limp ragdolls. — [Wikipedia: Euphoria (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_(software))
- A 2010 "Euphoria 3D White Paper: DMS" exists on Scribd (not fetched here; title only). — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/doc/36401342/Euphoria-3D-White-Paper-Dms)

**Behaviours: composition and tuning (MCV/Develop interview with NaturalMotion's Topping and Cavanagh, c. 2011-2012)**
- Euphoria is "an optional add-on to Morpheme that generates character movement at runtime on the CPU," simulating "the nervous system, body, and muscles of characters so they can react to their surroundings." Behaviours are "just a framework a developer puts direction into." — [MCV/Develop: Ecstatic movement](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)
- There are "tens" of behaviours; their strength is in combining them "like LEGO bricks." Each behaviour can be customised and tuned, many "can be modified by supplementary animations"; developers tune traits "such as strong, weak, timid, or forceful." Developers can override the system with "various forces and stronger constraints" to force a specific result. Topping: "more akin to a director's tool than what traditional keyframing allows." — [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)
- Euphoria is explicitly "not a replacement for IK" but "a complimentary technology": IK suits opening doors, locomotion, ladder climbing; Euphoria suits "high-quality reactive behaviours." Running a full physics rig is much more costly than two-bone IK. The commercial version required PhysX as the physics engine. Clumsy Ninja showed it could run on an iPad. — [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)

### Inferences
- **Architecture (reconstructed, not vendor-confirmed):** The API shows the pattern clearly. A behaviour is a named controller with up to 64 scalar/bool parameters, sent as a message to a physics rig; several run concurrently (the "LEGO" composition), and global messages (`ConfigureBalance`, `SetStiffness`, `SetMuscleStiffness`, `SetCharacterStrength/Health`) set shared gains that the behaviours read. `ActivePose`/`AnimPose` drive the rig toward an animated pose; "set stiffness before ActivePose drives to an animated pose" implies joint-space PD-style tracking of an animation target with tunable stiffness (the robotics notion of a "muscle" here is almost certainly a per-joint PD/servo with stiffness and damping, not a Hill-type muscle; no public source states this explicitly).
- **Intent encoding:** Intent lives in the behaviour set, not in a cost function the game sees: `StayUpright`/`BodyBalance` (stay standing), `BraceForImpact`/`CatchFall` (protect the head, get the arms out), `HeadLook`/`ShotHeadLook` (look at the threat), `PointGun`/`FireWeapon` (keep aiming while hit), `Grab`/`Yanked` (hold on). "Cheat torques" (ShotShockSpin) and "various forces and stronger constraints" show that non-physical helper forces are a normal part of the toolkit. Character traits (strong/weak/timid) are parameter presets.
- **Blending with animation:** Three regimes are implied: (1) pure animation; (2) animation-driven physics, where `ActivePose`/`AnimPose` make the rig track the animation with set stiffness (used to enter/exit ragdoll with intent); (3) behaviour-driven physics (Shot, HighFall, Stagger, Balance) where the rig is fully authoritative and animation is only a reference for some limbs ("many behaviours can be modified by supplementary animations"). Rockstar's "stunt falls" and horse-dragging in RDR2 are regime (3) triggered by gameplay events. `StopAllBehaviors` and `BodyRelax` are the exits to limp ragdoll.
- **Life vs realism:** Euphoria is a REALISM/ADAPTABILITY mechanism at its core (physically correct reaction to unscripted forces) that is deliberately styled toward LIFE by hand-tuned, film-referenced behaviour parameters (the "director's tool", the action-film comments in the config). The vendor's own 2006 claim that it would replace mocap did not happen; by 2012 they described it as a complement to IK and keyframes.
- **In/Out table (reconstructed):** In = physics rig state (joint angles/velocities, contacts), game events (shot, impulse, grab target), per-behaviour parameters, optional animation target pose and stiffness, environment sensor queries. Out = joint torques/targets for the physics rig each tick (plus cheat forces), i.e. a full-body pose produced by simulation, which the renderer skins directly.
- **Status inference:** Because Euphoria ships inside RAGE's source (per Wikipedia) and Rockstar is the only known user after licensing ended in 2017, it is most likely maintained in-house by Rockstar for RDR2 and later titles; GTA VI use is plausible but unconfirmed.

The NaturalMotion patent text was not retrieved; the 2010 Scribd white paper was not read.
- Rockstar has published no technical talk on RAGE animation; the "Animation Pipeline at Rockstar" page at `rockstar-files.pages.dev` surfaced in search but its provenance is unknown (possibly from leaked documents) and it was not used.
- No RDR2-specific list of behaviour message names was found; the GTA V list is the best proxy and RDR2 modders say many imported Max Payne 3 parameters do not work, implying the set changed.
- Whether Euphoria is in GTA VI is unconfirmed; Rockstar has not said.

## Q2. RDR2 locomotion and animation blending: what is publicly known?

### Takeaway
Rockstar has confirmed only that RDR2's animation system was "radically overhauled" and that Euphoria is layered under other systems; the rest of what is "known" is third-party measurement (Dan Lowe's frame-count comparison, Digital Foundry's latency tests) and the widely reported observation that the controls feel heavy because animation and momentum, not just input latency, gate responses.

### Cited Findings
- Hooker (Rockstar North): Euphoria is "just a part of how we simulate and animate characters"; the whole animation system was "radically overhauled" for more accurate human-like and animal-like behaviour; horses specifically get Euphoria reactions when shot or colliding with vehicles, trees and buildings. — [Wccftech/VG247](https://wccftech.com/rockstar-euphoria-evolved-rdr2/)
- Dan Lowe (animator, EA Motive) compared input-to-response times across RDR2, Spider-Man, Assassin's Creed Odyssey, Uncharted: The Lost Legacy and Destiny 2; RDR2 was slowest, with Arthur needing up to 11 frames to respond, roughly twice the next game; the coverage notes the method is not wholly conclusive. — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=372357); [Mein-MMO (EN)](https://mein-mmo.de/en/red-dead-redemption-2-experte-steuerung-traege,288771)
- Lowe: typical shooter characters accelerate to about 4.5 m/s within roughly 10 frames; Rockstar instead chose a more realistic movement model that "supports high-quality animation" but "just comes at the expense of game feel"; players have adapted to arcade-like response so realistic motion reads as sluggish. — [Cartoon Brew (Amid Amidi summarising Lowe)](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/educational/an-analysis-of-animation-systems-in-red-dead-redemption-2-166968.html)
- Push Square (Sammy Barker, 15 Nov 2018): same-TV, same-console comparison vs Spider-Man and AC Odyssey; concludes the sluggishness is mainly a deliberate creative choice to convey weight, pointing to "exaggerated, hefty animations"; "not clear, scientific proof by any stretch." — [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2018/11/red_dead_redemption_2s_sluggish_controls_analysed)
- Reports summarising Lowe say input lag is not the sole culprit: many interactions are tied to long animations that make the game feel slower. — [Mein-MMO (EN)](https://mein-mmo.de/en/red-dead-redemption-2-experte-steuerung-traege,288771)
- Digital Foundry (reported by Wccftech) measured end-to-end input latency of 385 ms on Stadia, 355 ms on PC and 435 ms on Xbox One X for RDR2 (different setup from Lowe's frame counts). — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/red-dead-redemption-2-stadia-less-latency-xbox-one-x/)
- Informal community estimate: about 14 frames at 30 fps for walking input, citing Lowe's chart (not a verified measurement). — [ResetEra thread](https://resetera.com/threads/should-games-journalists-talk-about-the-red-dead-killzone-effect-in-their-reviews.90969/)
- Steam users describe keyboard movement continuing after key release, with replies saying the character "has momentum" (anecdotal). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1174180/discussions/0/5017567849304448594)
- RDR2 Euphoria/physics behaviours are configured in `physicstasks.ymt` (see Q1). — [Nexus Mods](https://nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/1675)

### Inferences
- **Structure (reconstructed):** The consistent picture across measurements is an animation-first mover: the visible character's displacement is driven by the selected animation (start/stop/turn transitions play to completion, momentum is carried in the clip), with gameplay velocity lagging the stick. This is the "synchronise simulation to character" choice in Holden's taxonomy (Q3), which trades responsiveness for weight and no foot sliding; Lowe's "realistic movement model" is the same observation from the animator's side. The Mover/stance/holster layer names in the question could not be confirmed from public sources.
- **Life vs realism:** RDR2's locomotion choice is a LIFE/CHARACTER decision (weight, "an ordinary man") paid for with responsiveness; the Euphoria layer underneath is ADAPT (reaction to shots, collisions, horse falls).
- **In/Out (reconstructed):** In = stick vector, context (stance, weapon, mount, terrain), animation database; Out = root displacement taken from the animation plus full-body pose; Euphoria takes over on impact events and hands back via ActivePose-style recovery (Q1).

### Gaps
- No Rockstar technical talk or article on the RDR2 animation stack (mover, blend trees vs motion matching, horse rig, stance/holster layering) exists publicly; the terms "Mover" and layer names are community/internal vocabulary and could not be sourced.
- Lowe's original video was not retrieved; frame counts are secondhand from press summaries.
- Nothing public on how horse locomotion is built beyond Hooker's Euphoria examples.

## Q3. Motion matching: algorithm, what it replaced, data and runtime, limits, Learned Motion Matching, displacement, neural successors

### Takeaway
Motion matching (Clavet, Ubisoft Montreal, GDC 2016, shipped in For Honor) replaces the hand-built state machine/blend-tree hierarchy with a brute-force search every few frames over long unstructured mocap takes for the frame that best matches the current pose and the desired future trajectory, followed by a short blend (now usually inertialization); its costs are memory and mocap coverage, which Learned Motion Matching (Holden et al. 2020) attacks by replacing each step with networks, and which PFNN/MANN/NSM/DeepPhase attack by learning a generative controller outright.

### Cited Findings

**The algorithm as presented in 2016**
- GDC Vault abstract (Clavet, Ubisoft Montreal, GDC 2016, Programming track): instead of "arranging many small animations within a large state structure," the team uses a declarative approach, adding structured markup to long clips only for meaning that cannot be inferred from motion (attack categories, defensive stances); starts, stops and turns are not built by hand; "several minutes of motion-capture footage of someone moving around are imported directly into the engine"; at runtime "the system continually searches the motion-capture database for the frame that best matches both the current pose and the intended upcoming movement," then "blends briefly into that point in the data"; result "looks nearly identical to raw mocap while remaining responsive enough for comfortable player control." — [GDC Vault 1022985](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022985/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road)
- Clavet called it a "ridiculously brute-force approach to animation selection." Each candidate mocap segment is scored with a cost function; pose-matching and trajectory-matching costs are precomputed offline and stored as metadata. Pose features: a few bones only (not the full skeleton), local velocity, foot positions and velocities (relaxed for turns, where foot-shuffling is common), weapon positions, about 10 factors in all. Trajectory features: desired stance, future position, future orientation, future velocity/acceleration; each unmatched factor adds cost. The desired trajectory comes from code: a spring-damper on desired velocity produces a simulated point and the character's centre of mass is clamped to within 15 cm of it. A "realism-versus-comfort" slider sets fidelity vs immediacy. The system can anticipate walls/ledges and pick clips that avoid them. If no good match exists it blends between anims. Timescale limits: no more than 10% faster or 20% slower. Toe locking at runtime was needed because rapid clip switching caused foot sliding. Stopping takes about 1 m; Clavet argued consistency matters more than raw responsiveness. Over 100 characters on screen with aggressive LOD. Two-character moves (throws) are handled by animation-driven displacement, not matching. — [Game Anim write-up of Clavet's GDC 2016 talk](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=13538)
- "Dance cards" = the checklist a mocap performer runs through: walks/runs, small repositions, starts/stops, circles/turns, plant-and-turns at 45/90/135/180 degrees, square strafing with 90-degree plants, strafe plants for 180-degree direction changes; about 5 minutes of exploratory mocap; capture is limited mainly by performer stamina, so fatigued motion is captured late. Clips are tagged (Tired, Heavy Attack, Stance, Range, Block, Parry) and marked with events (stance change, sword contact). Four or five Ubisoft projects were using motion matching at the time. — [Game Anim write-up](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=13538)
- Japanese GDC 2016 coverage: the full mocap dataset is searched every frame for the best matching frame; the main cost is a very large mocap library, and the For Honor team ran repeated mocap sessions weekly to fill gaps. — [Game Watch Impress](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/749136.html)
- The "history" Clavet gave of what it replaced: play-anim, state machines, blend/decision trees, bone masks, per-state blend trees, parametric blends, anim lists; the old pipeline required manually chopping takes into cycles and categorising every variant such as "Start-Strafe90-TurnOnSpot45-Stop." Stated pros: mocap quality preserved, controllable responsiveness, minimal manual work (time from mocap to game), characters can share no animations even with similar move-sets. Memory requirements were deferred to a future talk. — [Game Anim write-up](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=13538)
- Kristjan Zadziuk's name is attached to the GDC 2016 "Motion Matching and The Road to Next-Gen Animation" session in the question; GDC Vault lists two entries with that title (1022985 and 1023280); only 1022985 (Clavet) was confirmed in this session. — [GDC Vault 1023280](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023280/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road); [GDC Vault 1022985](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022985/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road)

**Inertialization (the transition method that replaced cross-fade blending)**
- David Bollo (Microsoft/The Coalition), GDC 2018, "Inertialization: High-Performance Animation Transitions in 'Gears of War'": presents the mathematics for both vector and quaternion values and how to add it to an existing engine; the team "decided to avoid blended transitions" and handle transitions "as a post-process." — [GDC Vault 1025165](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025165/Inertialization-High-Performance-Animation-Transitions); [Game Developer video post](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/video-high-performance-animation-transitions-in-i-gears-of-war-i-)
- Bollo's SIGGRAPH 2017 talk "High Performance Animation in Gears of War 4" gives the figure of an animation post-processing step that is 60% cheaper to compute overall. — [SIGGRAPH History](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=84136)
- A 2026 arXiv paper ("Half Pound Filter for Real-Time Animation Blending") notes inertialization "overcome[s] partially" an overshoot issue by setting the projected velocity to zero if it would move the value further from the target. — [arXiv 2602.21702](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21702)
- Related Coalition talk: "Motion Warping in Gears of War 4" (GDC 2017). — [GDC Vault 1024219](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024219/Motion-Warping-in-Gears-of)

**Learned Motion Matching (Holden, Kanoun, Perepichka, Popa; ACM TOG 39(4), SIGGRAPH 2020)**
- Authors: Daniel Holden and Oussama Kanoun (Ubisoft La Forge), Maksym Perepichka and Tiberiu Popa (Concordia). The memory use of motion matching "generally scales linearly with the amount of data used"; the paper breaks motion matching "into its individual steps" and replaces each with "learned, scalable alternatives"; "our final model has no need to store animation data or additional matching meta-data in memory," while keeping motion matching's "quality, control, and quick iteration time"; motion matching is valued for "flexibility, predictability, low pre-processing time, and visual quality." — [Ubisoft Montreal / La Forge](https://montreal.ubisoft.com/?p=23009); [La Forge news](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/news/3NWqwcMU9lMfumgfPAEfka/learned-motion-matching); [PDF](https://www.theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)
- A later Ubisoft interview says the work had not yet been integrated into a shipped game but was the basis for the "Choreograph" tool that helped animate Far Cry 6. — [Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/27176jslYNMPt7vBfCaRQ1)
- Paper Digest ranks it a "Most Influential SIGGRAPH 2020 paper" (rank 15, 2026-03 edition). — [Paper Digest](https://www.paperdigest.org/certificate/230450cec61c236e)

**Holden, "Code vs Data Driven Displacement" (theorangeduck.com)**
- Two entities: a code-driven *simulation object* (capsule) whose desired velocity comes from the gamepad through a critically damped spring, with separate forward/sideways/backward speeds; and the data-driven *character entity*, which must move as its animation does or it floats and its feet slide. — [theorangeduck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- The *simulation bone*: an extra root bone added to the mocap representing the simulation object; generated by projecting an upper-spine bone to the ground and smoothing with a Savitzky-Golay filter (position), and projecting hip forward direction to the ground (rotation). Works best on long takes starting and ending in a standing pose. Numbers from the data: running forward just over 4 m/s, strafing just under 3 m/s, backward about 2 m/s; walking forward about 1.75 m/s, sideways 1.5 m/s, backward 1.25 m/s; the simulation object's statistics largely overlapped the animation data. — [theorangeduck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- Options for the mismatch: (1) do nothing and let motion matching match the future trajectory of the simulation object (simple, imprecise, can drift into walls); (2) snap the character to the simulation each frame (one-to-one control but foot sliding, floatiness, spinning); (3) snap the simulation to the character (little sliding, more weight, but sluggish); (4) blend; (5) per-frame damped adjustment, optionally velocity-limited (example ratio 0.5), correcting rotation only while turning and position only while moving fast; (6) clamping deviation to a max distance/angle (predictable bounds for networking/collision but can feel "dragged along"). Further tools: retime/mirror data (example: speed mocap up about 10%); let animators label regions of data with the desired displacement behaviour ("data-centric control"); instant procedural head look-at for perceived responsiveness; foot locking with an inertializer plus IK as a last resort ("a little sliding often looks better than poorly posed limbs"); over-the-shoulder camera to hide feet. Conclusion: data-driven methods like motion matching "are not inherently opposed to fast, code-driven control" if the database is large and interruptible. — [theorangeduck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)

**Neural successors (all from the AI4Animation repository and Holden's page)**
- PFNN (Holden, Komura, Saito, SIGGRAPH 2017, TOG 36(4) Art. 42): the regression network's weights are generated each frame by a cyclic function of a *phase* variable; inputs are "user controls, the previous state of the character, the geometry of the scene" plus phase; trained end-to-end on a large locomotion dataset (walking, running, jumping, climbing) fitted into virtual terrains; needs "only milliseconds of execution time and a few megabytes of memory, even when trained on gigabytes of motion data"; the authors argue the explicit phase is why it beats LSTM-style autoregression. — [theorangeduck PFNN page](https://theorangeduck.com/page/phase-functioned-neural-networks-character-control); [paper PDF](https://www.ipab.inf.ed.ac.uk/cgvu/phasefunction.pdf)
- Mode-Adaptive Neural Networks (Zhang and Starke joint first authors, Komura, Saito, SIGGRAPH 2018, TOG 37(4) Art. 145): for quadrupeds; instead of fixed weights the network blends groups of expert weights "based on the current state of the character"; learns end-to-end without phase or gait labels from unstructured quadruped mocap. — [AI4Animation](https://github.com/sebastianstarke/AI4Animation)
- Neural State Machine (Starke and Zhang joint first authors, Komura, Saito, SIGGRAPH Asia 2019, TOG 38(6) Art. 178): one compact model for character-scene interactions (sitting, carrying, opening doors, obstacle avoidance) controlled "from simple control commands"; learns from scene geometry so motions adapt to scene variations. — [AI4Animation](https://github.com/sebastianstarke/AI4Animation)
- Local Motion Phases (Starke, Zhao, Komura, Zaman, SIGGRAPH 2020, TOG 39(4) Art. 54): per-limb/contact phases so multi-contact skills (basketball dribbling, catching, object interaction) are learned from unstructured data "without any manual labeling." — [AI4Animation](https://github.com/sebastianstarke/AI4Animation)
- Neural Animation Layering (Starke, Zhao, Zinno, Komura, SIGGRAPH 2021, TOG 40(4) Art. 92): networks that "imitate animation layering" to mix/blend/edit martial-arts motion from unaligned sources. — [AI4Animation](https://github.com/sebastianstarke/AI4Animation)
- DeepPhase (Starke, Mason, Komura, SIGGRAPH 2022, TOG 41(4) Art. 136): a periodic autoencoder learns a multi-dimensional phase manifold from unstructured motion without labels; applications include locomotion, style, dance from music, football dribbling, pose queries. — [AI4Animation](https://github.com/sebastianstarke/AI4Animation)
- Categorical Codebook Matching (Starke, Starke, He, Komura, Ye, SIGGRAPH 2024, TOG 43(4) Art. 142): learns a motion manifold and sampling end-to-end by matching categorical codebooks for inputs and outputs; drives full-body avatars from sparse three-point tracking, with a hybrid mode adding joystick/goal control. A 2026 Python port (AI4AnimationPy) removes the Unity dependency. — [AI4Animation](https://github.com/sebastianstarke/AI4Animation)

### Inferences
- **Classification:** Motion matching is a LIFE/CHARACTER mechanism (it preserves the performer's idiosyncrasy, weight and style at mocap fidelity) with controllable responsiveness; it adds no ADAPT beyond what the database contains (wall/ledge anticipation is just cost terms over existing clips). Inertialization is a pure quality-of-transition tool (neutral; makes LIFE cheaper). Learned Motion Matching is an engineering fix for memory, not a change in capability. PFNN/NSM/DeepPhase trade a little fidelity for compact models that generalise across terrain/scene geometry, i.e. they add some ADAPT (terrain height input in PFNN, scene geometry in NSM) while still being bounded by training data.
- **In/Out:** Motion matching: In = current pose features (a few joints: positions/velocities, feet, hips) + desired future trajectory (positions, facings, 0.2-1 s ahead) from a code-driven simulated capsule + tags; Out = an index into the database (clip, frame), blended via inertialization into a full-body pose and root displacement. PFNN: In = trajectory window (past+future), terrain heights under it, previous pose, phase; Out = next pose, root velocity, phase increment. NSM: In = goal/action command, scene geometry volume, previous state; Out = next pose plus contact/goal progress.
- **What it cannot do (from the sources):** produce behaviours not in the database (every "turn at 135 degrees" was captured on purpose; weekly mocap sessions to fill gaps); exact foot placement on arbitrary geometry (foot sliding needed toe locking; Holden calls foot IK a "last resort"); exact displacement control (the 15 cm CoM clamp and the 1 m stop are the admitted slack); two-body contact moves (throws handled separately); and it does not react to physical perturbation at all, which is why studios layer physics/Euphoria-like systems on top.
- **Cost picture (from the sources):** precomputed feature metadata and a search every few frames over all candidate frames; memory linear in mocap minutes (the LMM motivation); 100+ characters with LOD in For Honor.

### Gaps
- Learned Motion Matching's internals (the three learned stages commonly called Decompressor/Stepper/Projector, compression ratios, runtime per character) could not be verified here: the PDF exceeded the fetch size limit and the La Forge pages are abstract-only. Treat those names as unverified in these notes.
- Exact matching cadence (every N frames), feature weights, and database sizes for For Honor and TLOU2 are not in the abstracts; Clavet explicitly deferred memory numbers.
- The Zadziuk talk's specific content (Ubisoft Toronto's perspective) was not retrieved; the Game Anim write-up covers only Clavet.
- EA's own motion-matching implementation (used in FIFA/EA SPORTS FC and others) was not researched in this session.

## Q4. Parkour/climbing (Assassin's Creed, Uncharted, Zelda BotW, Dying Light): how hands and feet are placed on uneven geometry, what is authored vs procedural

### Takeaway
Across the public sources the pattern is the same: climbable geometry is detected (raycasts/annotations), a short authored or mocap transition is selected and warped to the target, and procedural IK then "fixes up" hand and foot contacts; Ubisoft's two published contributions are HumanIK for AC II (2009) and a predictive, biomechanically modelled foot IK for AC (GDC 2016), while the AC III team reported rejecting most physics-based procedural techniques because characters "seemed to react mindlessly."

### Cited Findings
- GDC 2013 Animation Bootcamp (Ubisoft Montreal animation director, as summarised by Wolfire): Assassin's Creed climbing "relies on IK for hand and foot placement, with an added procedural layer for overall body movement for reacting to jumps and landings"; the procedural animation team "tried many physics-based techniques but kept only a few of them" and "most were rejected because the characters seemed to react mindlessly to the environment." (Summary page was not reachable at fetch time; quotes come from the search snippet of that page.) — [Wolfire blog GDC13 summary part 4/6](https://wolfire-blog.appspot.com/2013/04/GDC13-Summary-Animation-Bootcamp-Part-4-6)
- GDC 2016, "Fitting the World: A Biomechanical Approach to Foot IK", Clifford Roche and Carlos Torres-Cros (Ubisoft), Programming track: traditional foot IK "reacts to changes in the environment's physical layout," which gives slow responses to fast knee movements and procedural-looking motion; for Assassin's Creed's organic environments Ubisoft moved to a "predictive approach based on biomechanical behavior" that better preserves the original animation and looks more natural, fluid and accurate. — [GDC Vault 1023009](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023009)
- Autodesk (2009): HumanIK middleware was integrated into Ubisoft's engine for Assassin's Creed II; a lead programmer credited it with letting the team iterate on climbing moves "without worrying about IK solving quality." — [CGW / Autodesk press](https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/News/2009/Autodesk-HumanIK-Middleware-Helps-Assassins-Cree.aspx); [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/?p=132628)
- AC Unity (2014): producer Lesley Phord-Toy said Unity merged the previously separate climbing and free-running systems into one parkour system; GameSpot reported Unity "completely rebuilds" parkour, stealth and traversal. — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=203464); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/assassins-creed-unity-completely-rebuilds-the-seri/1100-6421616/)
- AC Odyssey: Ubisoft said Ancient Greece lacked tall buildings, so the "climbing-frame" design had to be reinvented around smaller points of interest (level-design, not animation, statement). — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/assassins-creed/one-problem-with-making-an-assassins-creed-game-in-ancient-greece-says-ubisoft-is-that-there-just-werent-enough-tall-buildings-for-a-climbing-frame-game)
- Opinion coverage argues parkour animation quality peaked with Unity and that Origins/Odyssey/Valhalla have stiffer transitions (critic opinion, not developer statement). — [Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/why-assassins-creed-parkour-peaked-with-unity-and-hasnt-been-the-same-since)
- Uncharted (per a forum post citing a Naughty Dog animation talk): "Procedural animations like IK are layered over the basic blends to 'fix up' the positions of hands and feet to match the environment." — [Unity forum: Ledge Grab Mechanics](https://discussions.unity.com/t/ledge-grab-mechanics/560844)
- Generic ledge-grab pipeline (industry guide): the character is first moved to a precise offset from the ledge computed from raycast hit data so the hands land correctly, then IK fine-tunes contact; "keyframe animation drives the broad strokes of movement while procedural IK handles the fine detail of contact points." — [Mocap Online climbing guide](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/climbing-animation-games-guide)
- A real-time-animation tutorial distinguishes "anchor" (static) climbing, where the character snaps to fixed anchor points as in Uncharted-style systems, from "dynamic" climbing as in Zelda: Breath of the Wild, where the character does not snap to fixed anchors. — [Realtime Animation Tips](https://sites.google.com/view/realtime-animation-tips/tutorials/animation-third-person-climbing)
- A video analysis of BotW's climbing argues it is deliberately "stripped down and simple," while Uncharted and most Assassin's Creed games give characters more physicality in the climb (fan analysis; transcript fetch returned 403). — [Amara transcript listing](https://amara.org/videos/0IXJ0B4bKKKN/en/3103528)
- Dying Light: Techland's "Natural Movement" system handles parkour running, jumping and climbing; Bartosz Kulon (Techland) gave a GDC 2018 talk on how it was built, with problem/solution examples (the summary page gives no algorithmic detail). Dying Light 2 "simulates scanning in real time" rather than relying on precomputed data, and the player grabs ledges they can reach "as long as you're looking at them"; Techland consulted David Belle. — [Game Developer (Kulon GDC 2018)](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-how-techland-built-i-dying-light-i-s-remarkable-parkour-system); [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/techland-explains-how-dying-light-2-parkour-works); [GameWatcher 2014](https://www.gamewatcher.com/news/2014-01-09-dying-light-developers-explain-their-natural-movement-system)

### Inferences
- **Reconstructed AC pipeline:** (1) environment is annotated or scanned for grab points/edges (the "climbing-frame" design vocabulary and Odyssey's complaint about building size imply authored/annotated affordances rather than pure geometry scanning); (2) an authored transition (mocap or keyframe) is chosen and its root motion warped so the end pose lands on the target; (3) HumanIK/full-body IK pins hands and feet; (4) a small procedural layer adds landing/jump body reaction; (5) foot IK is predictive (planned from the animation's biomechanics) rather than reactive. BotW is the other extreme: a general surface-following controller with sparse authored poses, trading physicality for "climb anything."
- **Classification:** Climbing/parkour systems are ADAPT mechanisms (fit the body to arbitrary geometry) that borrow LIFE from the authored clips they warp. Ubisoft's explicit rejection of physics-based reaction because it looked "mindless" is a direct statement that unmotivated physical adaptation reads as lifeless.
- **In/Out:** In = candidate contact points (annotations or raycasts), target pose constraints, current pose/velocity, player direction. Out = selected transition clip + root warp + per-limb IK targets (hands/feet) + body-lean offsets.
- **Robot relevance:** "predictive foot IK" (plan the contact from the gait's own biomechanics) is the animation analogue of planning footsteps ahead rather than correcting after contact.

- Dying Light's ledge detection specifics (raycast layout, hand placement) are in the GDC 2018 video, which was not retrieved.
- Uncharted's own talk (the one the forum post cites) was not identified.

## Q5. Other "feel" systems: Overgrowth, Rain World, The Last Guardian's Trico, Spider-Man, Ghost of Tsushima

### Takeaway
The small-team examples show that very few keyframes plus physics and procedural layering can produce convincing, responsive characters (Overgrowth: 13 keyframes per report; Rain World: nearly all creature motion from code), while big-budget Trico shows the opposite lesson: Ueda's team found simple played-back authored motions sometimes landed better than hard-won procedural ones, and that natural gaze and powerful poses are hard to get procedurally.

### Cited Findings
- GDC 2014 Animation Bootcamp, "An Indie Approach to Procedural Animation", David Rosen (Wolfire Games): "simple procedural techniques can create interactive, fluid animations with very few keyframes," with examples from Overgrowth, Receiver and Black Shades, and the question "what distinguishes a playable character from a vehicle?" The talk is free on GDC Vault. — [GDC Vault 1020583](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020583); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation)
- A forum summary says the Overgrowth character in the talk uses about 13 keyframes in total for everything, with responsiveness built into physics first and animation layered on top (secondhand). — [Unity forum](https://discussions.unity.com/t/an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation-gdc-video-talk/538228)
- Rain World: almost all creatures including the slugcat "operate on a system of procedural animation" computed by the physics engine rather than premade sequences; creator Joar Jakobsson: "I never really set out to do procedural animation," he just needed a way to move the slugcat's limbs; GDC 2016 Animation Bootcamp talk by Jakobsson and James Therrien; creatures are "animated with a combination of code and traditional animation," making them "soft and bendable, and responsive to their surroundings"; the same fluid approach extends into creature AI pursuing their own survival. — [Game Developer: Animating Rain World](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/video-animating-i-rain-world-i-and-its-many-squishy-stretchy-creatures); [Game Developer: ecosystem](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_World)
- The Last Guardian (CEDEC 2017, Masanobu Tanaka, genDESIGN animation director, via Game Anim write-up): each frame runs AI, then animation, procedural animation, physics, fur, rendering. No mocap; all keyframe. Procedural elements: body curve follows the spine and is exaggerated on steep turns; foot landing points are computed when the foot lifts; legs are kept from being displaced by obstacles and Trico ducks under low ceilings; head look-at moves head first with the neck following "snake-like," rotation axis near the nose tip, and the head avoids walls so as not to block the AI's line of sight; catching a thrown barrel predicts its arc. AI: objects have interest priorities, interaction lowers interest; point-of-interest, then move-to-interest, then action decision, with action chosen by surrounding space. Early look-at tests looked too mechanical. Trico deliberately avoids fully automatic generation, always adjusting authored animation. — [Game Anim: Trico CEDEC 2017](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=15474)
- Tanaka: "it's quite difficult to produce a natural gaze or powerful pose with procedural animation"; Ueda: some "ready-made motions that could be played back without much trouble had a bigger impact on players than some of the parts that we worked hard to implement with procedural animation." — [genDESIGN interview](https://www.gendesign.co.jp/qa_01/interview01en.html)
- Marvel's Spider-Man (Insomniac): GDC 2019 "Concrete Jungle Gym: Building Traversal" (Doug Sheahan) covers how swinging was built and evolved for usability; GDC 2024 "Thwips and Hugs: The Animation of Marvel's Spider-Man 2" (Lindsay Thompson et al.). No source found describing the locomotion-warping internals. — [GDC Vault 1026084](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026084/Concrete-Jungle-Gym-Building-Traversal); [GDC Vault 1034543](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034543/Animation-Summit-Thwips-and-Hugs)
- Ghost of Tsushima: GDC 2021 talks cover wind simulation ("Blowing from the West", Bill Rockenbeck) and open-world systems, not locomotion animation. — [GDC Vault 1027124](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027124/Blowing-from-the-West-Simulating); [GDC news](https://gdconf.com/news/see-how-sucker-punch-brought-island-tsushima-life-gdc-2021)

### Inferences
- **Classification:** Overgrowth and Rain World are LIFE-through-physics systems with tiny data budgets: a handful of authored poses give the character, and a physics/spring layer gives responsiveness and contact adaptation (ADAPT as a side effect). Trico is a LIFE system where the authored keyframes carry the character and procedural layers (gaze, foot targets, ceiling ducking, catch prediction) add ADAPT; the team's own verdict is that procedural gaze/power poses undershoot authored ones.
- **In/Out:** Overgrowth-style: In = a few keyframe poses, controller state, physics (velocity, contacts); Out = interpolated pose with physics-driven secondary motion and foot placement. Trico: In = AI goal/interest object, authored clip, environment (ceiling height, obstacle); Out = clip with procedurally adjusted spine curve, head/neck chain, foot targets.

- Rosen's talk slides were not retrieved; the "13 keyframes" and "physics first" claims are forum summaries.
- Rain World GDC 2016 slides/devlogs not retrieved; technical detail is press-level.
- Nothing authoritative on Spider-Man's locomotion warping or Ghost of Tsushima's locomotion stack was found.

## Lessons for a layered robot controller (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Game studios converged on the same stack a biped controller needs: a code-driven "simulation object" for responsiveness, a data-driven layer for character, and a physics/behaviour layer for reaction, with explicit, tunable slack between them.

### Cited Findings
- See Q1 (behaviour composition, stiffness/strength messages), Q3 (simulation bone, 15 cm clamp, responsiveness slider, foot locking as last resort), Q4 (predictive foot IK; "mindless" physics rejected), Q5 (Trico's authored-beats-procedural verdict).

### Inferences
- Keep three layers with explicit interfaces: (a) a code-driven intent/capsule (desired velocity through a critically damped spring, separate gains for translation and heading), (b) a data/clip layer that supplies the character's gait and style, (c) a physics-reactive layer with a small set of composable, parameterised behaviours (stayUpright, catchFall, braceForImpact, headLook) with global stiffness/strength knobs.
- Make the slack between intent and body an explicit, tuned parameter (Clavet's 15 cm CoM clamp, Holden's velocity-limited adjustment with ratio 0.5), not an accident; measure it like Dan Lowe did (frames from input to visible response).
- Prefer predictive contact planning (plan the foot target when the foot lifts, as Trico and Ubisoft's predictive foot IK do) over reactive IK correction; accept a little slip over badly posed limbs.
- Behaviours should carry intent (protect head, keep looking at the target, keep the "gun" pointed) rather than only physical correctness; Ubisoft dropped physics reactions that looked mindless, and NaturalMotion exposed trait presets (strong/weak/timid) so the same controller reads as a character.
- Authored motion is cheap and lands with people (Ueda); reserve procedural generation for what must adapt (contacts, gaze, perturbation recovery), and keep a hard exit (StopAllBehaviors/BodyRelax) and a re-entry path (ActivePose with set stiffness) between the clip layer and the physics layer.
- Data layers only reproduce what was captured: enumerate the "dance card" (starts, stops, 45/90/135/180 plants, strafes) for the robot's gait library deliberately, and budget capture/retraining for gaps.
