# Borrow the animation stack, keep one tracker

Unreal Engine 5, Unity and Rockstar's RAGE all run the same per-frame pipeline: a code-driven layer measures intent and state, a selector picks an authored clip (state machine, blend space or motion matching), one continuity post-process (inertialization) smooths every switch, shaping and grounding stages bend the clip to the measured world (orientation and stride warping, distance matching, foot IK), and a physics layer with 0-to-1 dials adds compliance or takes over (Unreal's RigidBody and Physical Animation nodes, Unity's PuppetMaster, RAGE's Euphoria behaviours). **Life lives in the authored clips and in cheap additive layers (idle breaks, breathing, look-at, timing knobs); realism lives in the post-processes and the physics dials.** Your three-layer design (joint-owning foundation with balance, a library of controls, a governor with self-correction) matches that separation of concerns, and the robot literature supports every layer. It pushes back on one point: the controls should be **data (reference clips plus a small command vector) tracked by one shared policy, not separately trained networks that a governor hot-swaps**. In the only head-to-head benchmark, a tracking policy whose training never saw cross-skill transitions succeeded on **2% of switches versus 100%** when transitions were trained ([Switch, arXiv 2604.14834](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14834v1)), and Disney, who shipped the per-clip-policy design on the BD-X droids, lists "single-policy learning for multiple skills" as its own next step ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). Transitions, not controls, are where every surveyed robot fell. On sim-to-real, the dominant gap for this servo class is the actuator, and the single most important finding is an **undocumented STS3215 acceleration cap (RAM register 85, factory value 50, measured 6 to 28 rad/s²)** that the BAM actuator model Open Duck uses does not include ([Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)); it leaves quiet standing untouched and slows exactly the fast recovery steps that misbehave on your hardware. The report ends with an ordered measurement plan whose pass criteria come from BAM (10 to 20 mrad trajectory error) and ToddlerBot (1.3° tracking error).

Throughout, **LIFE** marks an ingredient whose job is character and animator intent; **REALISM** marks one whose job is physical plausibility, responsiveness or adaptation to the world. The two are kept in separate tables and separate sub-sections so you can budget them separately.

## Three engines converge on one measure-select-blend-shape-ground pipeline

The question behind this section is simple: is there one architecture worth copying, or three? The answer is one. The engines differ in authoring tools and vocabulary, but the per-frame data flow is the same, and Disney's BD-X robot runs the same flow with the grounding and physics stages replaced by a learned policy.

**Unreal Engine 5.** An Animation Blueprint splits into a game-thread EventGraph that gathers state and a worker-thread AnimGraph, a tree of pose-producing nodes evaluated once per tick to a single Output Pose ([Epic docs, Animation Blueprints](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-blueprints-in-unreal-engine)). State machines give each state its own graph layer; transition rules are priority-ordered booleans whose `Blend Logic` is Standard Blend, Inertialization or Custom ([Epic docs, Transition Rules](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/transition-rules-in-unreal-engine)). Blend Spaces interpolate samples on one or two axes with spring-damper input smoothing ([Epic docs, Blend Spaces](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/blend-spaces-in-unreal-engine)); `Layered blend per bone` composites body regions by blend mask, which is how Lyra puts weapon fire on the upper body over full-body locomotion ([Epic docs, Animation in Lyra](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-in-lyra-sample-game-in-unreal-engine)). Sync Groups re-time clips by shared markers such as "Right Foot Down" so blended walks never cross feet ([Epic docs, Sync Groups](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-sync-groups-in-unreal-engine)).

Epic's reference structure computes measurement once in a base AnimBP, delegates clip choice to swappable linked layers or Chooser tables, and runs shaping (Orientation and Stride Warping, Distance Matching) and grounding (Foot Placement, Leg IK) as fixed post-process stages ([Epic docs, Animation in Lyra](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-in-lyra-sample-game-in-unreal-engine); [Epic docs, Game Animation Sample](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-animation-sample-project-in-unreal-engine)). The Game Animation Sample replaces the locomotion state machine with Motion Matching, production-ready since **UE 5.4 (April 2024)** after running on all Fortnite characters ([Epic blog, UE 5.4](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/blog/unreal-engine-5-4-is-now-available)), but keeps every downstream layer unchanged. Physics sits last, in three tiers: AnimDynamics (collision-free springs), the RigidBody node (component-space simulation with `Master Alpha` and velocity and acceleration clamps) and the Physical Animation Component (motor drives on real bodies with `orientation_strength`, `max_angular_force` and a 0-to-1 strength multiplier) ([Epic docs, Rigid Body](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-blueprint-rigid-body-in-unreal-engine); [Epic API, PhysicalAnimationData](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/PhysicalAnimationData)).

**Unity.** Mecanim's Animator Controller holds states (clips or blend trees); transitions fire on parameter conditions and normalized exit time, with an explicit interruption policy per transition ([Unity manual, Transitions](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/animation-section/animation-mecanim/animation-animator-controller/animation-state-machines/class-transition)). Layers composite by Avatar Mask in Override or Additive mode, higher layers taking precedence for the bones they mask ([Unity manual, Animation Layers](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/animation-section/animation-mecanim/animation-animator-controller/animation-state-machines/animation-layers)). Root motion is the robot-relevant abstraction: "The Body Transform is the mass center of the character", the Root Transform is its ground-plane projection, and the per-frame change in the root is what moves the object ([Unity manual, Root Motion](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/animation-section/animation-mecanim/avatar-creationand-setup/root-motion)). Post-processes run in a documented order: the built-in IK pass (four goals: two hands, two feet), then Animation Rigging constraints, which run "after its normal evaluation" of the Animator ([Unity Rigging Workflow](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.animation.rigging@1.3/manual/RiggingWorkflow.html)), then third-party LateUpdate solvers such as Final IK ([Final IK, FBBIK](http://www.root-motion.com/finalikdox/html/page8.html)), then PuppetMaster, which makes a ConfigurableJoint ragdoll follow the animated pose through Muscle, Pin and Mapping weights ([PuppetMaster Overview](http://www.root-motion.com/puppetmasterdox/html/page3.html)).

Two Unity facts matter for a robot builder. First, studios routinely bypass the graphical state machine: the Playables API lets code "dynamically create blend graphs and control blend weights" ([Unity manual, Playables](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/animation-section/animation-mecanim/playables)), and Animancer exists because the Animator Controller "can ignore or delay commands" and forces all logic into one asset ([Animancer, Why Replace Mecanim?](https://kybernetik.com.au/animancer/docs/introduction/mecanim-vs-animancer/why)). Second, Unity's own motion matching (Kinematica) stalled at 0.8-preview in August 2020 and never gained layering ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/what-happened-to-kinematica/862682)); the community answer is MxM, whose search runs every 1 to 3 frames against prediction points at 0.33, 0.66 and 1.0 s ([MxM technical reference](https://motion-matching-for-unity.readthedocs.io/en/latest/technical-reference/)).

**RAGE and Euphoria.** Rockstar publishes nothing technical, so everything here is vendor statements or community reverse engineering, and the architecture is reconstructed (inferred). Euphoria is NaturalMotion's runtime "Dynamic Motion Synthesis": "not based on canned data recall, but on fast simulations of the game character's actual motor control, muscles and biomechanics" ([Game Developer press release, 2006](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/naturalmotion-announces-euphoria-run-time-technology-for-next-generation-game-platforms)). It consists of "tens" of behaviours combined "like LEGO bricks", each tunable toward traits like "strong, weak, timid, or forceful", and NaturalMotion stated it is "not a replacement for IK" but a complement for "high-quality reactive behaviours" ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)). The GTA V scripting API exposes **86 behaviour messages**, among them `BodyBalance`, `StayUpright`, `CatchFall`, `BraceForImpact`, `HeadLook`, `PointGun`, `ActivePose` (drive the rig toward an animated pose), `SetStiffness`, `SetMuscleStiffness`, `ConfigureBalance` and `StopAllBehaviors`, each capped at 64 parameters ([SHVDN GTA.NaturalMotion docs](https://docs.ragecoop.com/SHVDN/GTA.NaturalMotion.html)). In RDR2 the behaviours live in one config file, `physicstasks.ymt` ([Nexus Mods](https://nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/1675)). NaturalMotion ended third-party licensing in June 2017 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/65937/naturalmotion-ends-commercial-tech-licensing-business/)); Rockstar's Phil Hooker said Euphoria is "just a part of how we simulate and animate characters" and that RDR2's animation system was "radically overhauled" ([Wccftech citing VG247](https://wccftech.com/rockstar-euphoria-evolved-rdr2/)). The muscle model, controller type and arbitration between behaviours commanding the same joint are not public (unverified); the message set implies joint-space PD tracking of an animated target with tunable stiffness (inferred).

RDR2's locomotion is the clearest example of an animation-first mover: animator Dan Lowe measured Arthur needing **up to 11 frames to respond**, roughly twice the next game, because Rockstar chose a "realistic movement model" that "supports high-quality animation" at "the expense of game feel" ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=372357); [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/educational/an-analysis-of-animation-systems-in-red-dead-redemption-2-166968.html)).

| Stage | Unreal Engine 5 | Unity | RAGE / Euphoria (reconstructed) | Disney BD-X robot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measure intent and state | EventGraph, `ThreadSafeUpdateAnimation`, Property Access | Script sets Speed, Grounded, Jump; `SpeedChangeRate` lerp | Stick vector, stance, weapon, mount (inferred) | Joystick layer, path frame, IMU |

| Layer compositing | Layered blend per bone, Slots, Linked Anim Layers | Override / Additive layers with Avatar Mask | Behaviours stacked (LEGO) | Background + triggered + joystick layers |
| Shape to measured motion | Orientation / Stride Warping, Distance Matching, Motion Warping | Root motion delta, `OnAnimatorMove` | Unknown | Command vector (velocity, turn rate, head offsets) |

## Life is authored and additive; realism is a stack of post-processes with dials

The second question is which parts of those pipelines make a character feel alive and which make it feel physically right. The engines never label nodes this way, so the classification below is this report's judgement, but it is consistent across all three stacks and across the robot papers.

| Ingredient | LIFE or REALISM | Where it lives in the engines | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authored clips, posture, asymmetric timing | LIFE | Sequence players, Montages, Timeline | Overwatch idles carry personality through stance and "perfect symmetry looks stiff" ([Babel notes on Gibson](https://babel.speldesign.uu.se/?p=113918)) |
| Idle breaks, breathing, sway | LIFE | `Apply Additive` with alpha; Unity Additive layer at low weight | Naughty Dog switched from partial to additive animation to add "sway in the arms and head" without stiffness ([Naughty Dog slides](https://www2.slideshare.net/naughty_dog/creating-a-character-in-uncharted-drakes-fortune)) |
| Look-at and attention | LIFE (target choice) over REALISM (solver) | Look At node, Multi-Aim, LookAtIK with clamps | Trico: every object has an interest value, "digested" after action, highest becomes focus ([CEDEC 2017 slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1cYDabTQGYe09w3PyUU4XLrDn2fHy9X5eJIFUjVDt89U/htmlpresent)) |
| Timing and amplitude knobs | LIFE | Play rate, `Cycle Offset`, Mirror | Playback speed shifts the read from bored to engaged ([Ribeiro and Paiva 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02898)) |
| Secondary motion springs | LIFE | AnimDynamics, Damped Transform, Dynamic Bone | Springs on appendages; "very stiff chain follows the animation almost exactly" |
| Blend spaces | LIFE samples on a REALISM axis | Blend Space, Blend Tree | Samples are how this character walks; the axis is measured speed |
| Transition continuity | REALISM in service of LIFE | Inertialization, Dead Blending, Blend Profiles | Velocity carried through the switch instead of lerping two moving targets ([Holden](https://theorangeduck.com/page/inertialization-transition-cost)) |
| Shaping to measured motion | REALISM | Orientation / Stride Warping, Distance Matching, Motion Warping | "Stride scale is locomotion speed divided by root motion speed" ([Epic docs, Pose Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine)) |
| Foot and hand grounding | REALISM | Foot Placement, Leg IK, FBIK, Grounder | AC "relies on IK for hand and foot placement" ([Wolfire GDC13 summary](https://wolfire-blog.appspot.com/2013/04/GDC13-Summary-Animation-Bootcamp-Part-4-6)) |
| Physics and reaction | REALISM styled toward LIFE | RigidBody, Physical Animation, PuppetMaster, Euphoria | Euphoria is a "director's tool" with trait presets ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/ecstatic-movement-naturalmotion-on-euphoria/)) |
| Motion matching | LIFE in the data, REALISM in the search | PoseSearch, MxM | "looks nearly identical to raw mocap while remaining responsive" ([GDC Vault, Clavet 2016](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022985/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road)) |

Two cross-cutting findings decide how much of each to buy. Ubisoft's AC III team "tried many physics-based techniques but kept only a few" because characters "seemed to react mindlessly to the environment" ([Wolfire GDC13 summary](https://wolfire-blog.appspot.com/2013/04/GDC13-Summary-Animation-Bootcamp-Part-4-6)): unmotivated physical adaptation reads as lifeless. And genDESIGN's Fumito Ueda found that "ready-made motions that could be played back without much trouble had a bigger impact on players than some of the parts that we worked hard to implement with procedural animation" ([genDESIGN interview](https://www.gendesign.co.jp/qa_01/interview01en.html)): authored motion is cheap and lands. Procedural layers prevent "stiff" and "dead"; authored clips supply "who".

### Blending between states: inertialization replaced the crossfade

A crossfade evaluates both source and destination for the whole blend. David Bollo's GDC 2018 talk on Gears of War 4 eliminated blended transitions and handled them "as a post-process with a technique called 'inertialization'" ([GDC Vault, Inertialization](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025331/Inertialization)). Holden's formulation: at the switch compute the offset between the previous output and the destination, then let it "decay to zero over the blend time, using either a critically damped spring or a cubic polynomial. The output is the incoming animation plus the decayed offset" ([Holden, Inertialization Transition Cost](https://theorangeduck.com/page/inertialization-transition-cost)). The state needed is one pose and one velocity; no second trajectory is evaluated. Bollo's exact quintic coefficients are login-walled and unverified here.

Epic's rules of thumb for inertialized transitions: "Keep duration under about 0.4 seconds. Avoid it when poses differ greatly" ([Epic docs, Transition Rules](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/transition-rules-in-unreal-engine)). For large pose differences Holden's Dead Blending (UE 5.3) extrapolates the outgoing clip forward with exponentially decaying velocity and crossfades into the new one; Epic's node ships with **default clamps of 500 cm/s translation and 360 deg/s rotation** ([Epic docs, Blend Nodes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-blueprint-blend-nodes-in-unreal-engine); [Holden, Dead Blending](https://theorangeduck.com/page/dead-blending)). Whether Dead Blending is officially production in 5.8 is unresolved: the doc page says experimental, a forum reply says stable since May 2024 ([Epic forums](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/stability-of-the-dead-blending-node-in-animation-workflows/2670429)).

Unity's contribution is phase alignment: the transition graph shows a white line of foot contact so "a smooth transition avoids foot slips, jumps in animation, and preserves the dynamics of the motion", and blend-tree clips must share timing ("the left foot hits at 0.0 and the right foot at 0.5") ([Unity manual, Transitions](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/animation-section/animation-mecanim/animation-animator-controller/animation-state-machines/class-transition); [Unity manual, Blend Trees](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/animation-section/animation-mecanim/animation-animator-controller/animation-state-machines/animation-blend-trees/class-blend-tree)). Unreal's marker-based sync does the same automatically ([Epic docs, Sync Groups](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-sync-groups-in-unreal-engine)). For a robot these are REALISM mechanics: inertialization is "remember an offset, let it decay" with the decay velocity clamped at actuator limits, and sync-by-phase is "switch gaits at a contact event", which is exactly what Disney does (walk-to-stand waits for the next double support) with ramps of **0.35 s for body motion and 0.1 s for show functions** ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)).

### Changing direction: warping the clip beats picking another clip

Games stopped authoring a clip per heading. Orientation Warping "rotates the leg IK bones toward the locomotion direction while twisting the spine to keep the facing angle", covering "up to 180 degrees of lower-body motion from one animation"; Stride Warping scales foot spacing so stride matches capsule speed ([Epic docs, Pose Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine)). Distance Matching retimes starts, stops and landings so "motion matches gameplay" ([Epic docs, Animation in Lyra](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-in-lyra-sample-game-in-unreal-engine)). Warping comes before IK because warping moves the foot targets and IK then solves the leg to them (inferred from the plugin's IK Rig prerequisite).

The "proper physical attributes" part is a deliberate slack between intent and body. Clavet's motion matching drives the character from a code-side spring-damper on desired velocity and clamps the centre of mass within **15 cm** of that simulated point; stopping takes about 1 m, timescale is limited to 10% faster or 20% slower, and "consistency matters more than raw responsiveness" ([Game Anim write-up of Clavet](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=13538)). Holden lays out the options between a code-driven capsule and a data-driven character: snap the character to the capsule (foot sliding, floatiness), snap the capsule to the character (weight, but sluggish), or a damped, velocity-limited adjustment (ratio 0.5, rotation adjusted more slowly); foot locking is "a last resort" and "a little sliding often looks better than poorly posed limbs" ([Holden, Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)). RDR2 sits at the far end of that slider (11 frames). The robot lesson is to make the slack a tuned parameter and measure it the way Lowe did, frames from input to visible response.

### Feet and hands on uneven things: authored transition, root warp, predictive IK

Assassin's Creed's documented recipe is authored climbing moves plus IK: AC II integrated Autodesk HumanIK so the team could iterate on moves "without worrying about IK solving quality" ([CGW / Autodesk](https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/News/2009/Autodesk-HumanIK-Middleware-Helps-Assassins-Cree.aspx)), and the GDC 2013 bootcamp described "IK for hand and foot placement, with an added procedural layer for overall body movement for reacting to jumps and landings" ([Wolfire GDC13 summary](https://wolfire-blog.appspot.com/2013/04/GDC13-Summary-Animation-Bootcamp-Part-4-6)). The 2016 refinement is the important one: reactive foot IK "reacts to changes in the environment's physical layout", which gives slow responses to fast knee movements, so Ubisoft moved to "a predictive approach based on biomechanical behavior" ([GDC Vault, Fitting the World](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023009)). Trico does the same: "a landing point is chosen when a foot lifts, then rechecked during the motion", and feet are pinned ([CEDEC 2017 slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1cYDabTQGYe09w3PyUU4XLrDn2fHy9X5eJIFUjVDt89U/htmlpresent)).

The engine primitives are Motion Warping, whose `Skew Warp` "warps root motion so the character matches the target's location and rotation by the window's end" (Epic's example is mantling, where "the warp window covers the hand placing on the obstacle") ([Epic docs, Motion Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)), then Leg IK and Foot Placement pinning feet after warping ([Epic docs, Game Animation Sample](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-animation-sample-project-in-unreal-engine)), with Full Body IK offering per-bone stiffness and per-axis limits (ankle Z −70 to 70 in Epic's example) ([Epic docs, Full Body IK](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/control-rig-full-body-ik-in-unreal-engine)). Final IK's Grounder shows the pelvis rule: each foot is offset by ground height, and if the offset is negative "the solver pulls the character down from the pelvis" rather than over-extending the leg ([Final IK, Grounder](http://www.root-motion.com/finalikdox/html/page9.html)). Every one of these needs a measured foothold; the duck has none, which is why the next section replaces warping with balance-variable foothold shifts.

## The duck's foundation and controls: one tracking policy, controls as data (REALISM)

This section maps your layers (1) and (2) and the self-correcting governor in (3) onto the evidence. The headline: the shape is right, the implementation unit is wrong. Controls must be clips and commands, not networks.

| Your layer | Game-engine analogue | Hardware precedent | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1) Foundation owning joints, PID-style, stands and balances | Unity ArticulationBody drive: `stiffness*(drive−target) − damping*(vel−targetVel)`; PuppetMaster muscle spring/damper | STS3215 P loop under a 50 Hz policy; Disney 50 Hz policy on a 600 Hz PD bus; NimbRo "pure proportional position control" | Supported, with two additions: setpoint clip plus low-pass, and a model-based standing fallback |
| (2) Library of controls, one per circumstance | Clip library, Blend Space samples, Montages | Disney clip library + nine gait samples; Cassie 7-gait HZD library; Walk These Ways 8-D behaviour vector | Supported as data; pushed back as separate networks |
| (3) Governor: when to switch, how to blend, when to recover | State machine with priorities and conduits; BehaviourPuppet supervisor; Choreographer track ownership | Switch scheduler (similarity thresholds + e-stop); Disney phase-gated switching; capture-step trigger | Supported as a small classical layer on one tracker |
| "Change one animation without retraining everything" | Swap a clip in a Chooser row | AMOR re-tunes weights after training in 1 day; Walk These Ways style inputs; BeyondMimic shared formulation | True for clip edits and style knobs; a new leg skill is one standard re-run of one job |

### Foundation: the servo P loop is the lowest layer, and it needs guards

With P-only servos the robot's foundation is physically a stiffness controller whose only per-joint knob is kp. The shipped Open Duck runtime runs **50 Hz, servo `pid=[30,0,0]`, head servos lowered to kp 8, `action_scale=0.25`**, with an optional `LowPassActionFilter` and a loop that warns "Policy control budget exceeded" when inference plus I/O passes 20 ms ([Open_Duck_Mini_Runtime, v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Mini_Runtime/blob/v2/scripts/v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py); local copy `sim/upstream_runtime/scripts/v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py`). Every servo biped surveyed keeps its policy or walk engine in position-target space and adds one of two guards: Disney clips setpoints "to a maximum deviation from measured position chosen so maximum torque can still be produced" and low-passes the 50 Hz hold at 37.5 Hz ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)); Peng et al. pass PD targets through a Butterworth low-pass on Laikago ([ar5iv 2004.00784](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.00784)). BeyondMimic prefers low-impedance PD on hardware because high gains "amplify sensor noise" ([arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4)).

The "understands standing and balance" half of your foundation already exists on the duck, but not as a separate layer: one joystick policy owns stand (zero command), walk, turn, head pose and push tolerance, with pushes every 5 to 10 s in training ([Open_Duck_Playground, joystick.py](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Playground/blob/main/playground/open_duck_mini_v2/joystick.py)). A classical standing fallback that needs no network is cheap and proven: ToddlerBot's teleop balance is a CoM controller plus an IMU torso-pitch PD ([arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v4)), and Disney's standing references are inverse-dynamics solutions with the CoP at the middle of the support polygon ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). Build it; it is the thing that stands when everything above it is broken.

Do not plan a torque-level or 200 Hz residual layer. The best residual-RL results (Yan et al. 2026, 100% success on Kangaroo, BRUCE and H1-2) are simulation-only, torque-controlled and 200 Hz ([arXiv 2601.16109](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16109v1)), three things the duck does not have.

### Controls: clips and commands, with leg joints owned by the tracker

The strongest evidence on what a "control authored for a physical robot" should contain is Disney's: "leg joint positions from the animation are ignored because they are not policy inputs. During walking, the lower body is determined entirely by the periodic policy and commanded path velocities" ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). The animation engine emits a **command vector** (head offsets plus torso pose while standing, head offsets plus path velocity and turn rate while walking, a phase for episodic stunts), and authored leg motion enters only through the imitation reward at training time, weighted **100 on the neck and 15 on the legs** ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). Disney's gait tool withholds pelvis horizontal translation from the animator so "a dynamic planner optimizes it to keep the center of pressure in the support polygon" ([US12293445B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12293445B2/en)). Boston Dynamics says the same in plainer words: animation "is still treated as a request to the balancing controller" ([Boston Dynamics, In Step with Spot](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/in-step-with-spot/)).

The duck already has the equivalent of Cassie's gait library. Cassie interpolated **seven HZD gaits** by measured sagittal velocity and needed a separate foot-placement regulator because "without it the closed loop is unstable" ([Gong et al., arXiv 1809.07279](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.07279)); the Open Duck reference generator sweeps a placo walk engine over (vx, vy, wz) and fits polynomials in phase ([Open_Duck_reference_motion_generator](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_reference_motion_generator)), and the training code indexes a 40-D reference frame by dx, dy, dtheta ([poly_reference_motion.py](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Playground/blob/main/playground/common/poly_reference_motion.py)). Disney blends its nine samples (forward fast/slow, step in place, reverse fast/slow, strafe left/right, turn left/right) "in parameter space on a gait-phase map" so contact events always align ([US12293445B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12293445B2/en)). If you want runtime style knobs (cadence, step height, stance width, trunk pitch), the only hardware-validated route is to feed them to the policy as inputs: Walk These Ways conditions one policy on an 8-D behaviour vector and "all gait parameters are consistently tracked after sim-to-real transfer" on a Go1 at 50 Hz ([ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)).

So each control becomes a reference clip or gait sample plus a command vector plus metadata (entry and exit phase, allowed blend partners), authored on a rig with exactly the robot's degrees of freedom, as Olaf's is: "The animation rig has the same degrees of freedom as the robot's articulated backbone, so references map directly" ([arXiv 2512.16705](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16705)). The Blender Servo Animation add-on exports per-bone servo positions and has a live mode ([blender-servo-animation](https://github.com/timhendriks93/blender-servo-animation)); Takayama's warning applies to any exporter: the Maya PR2 rig did not respect torso speed limits ([Takayama et al. 2011](https://www.wendyju.com/publications/151r-takayama.pdf)), so enforce joint velocity and acceleration limits before a clip reaches hardware.

### Where the evidence pushes back: one tracker, not many networks

Your phrase "like a neural network made of small, abstract, individually replaceable components" is where the robot literature disagrees most sharply, and the disagreement is specifically about networks, not about components. Switch's benchmark isolates the effect: a tracking policy trained on single-skill data succeeds on **2.00% of cross-skill transitions at every difficulty**, while the same policy class trained with transition-biased initialisation plus a skill graph reaches 100% ([arXiv 2604.14834](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14834v1)). HOVER's single multi-mode policy beats per-mode specialists on 11 of 12 real-world metrics on a Unitree H1 ([arXiv 2410.21229](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21229v2)); GMT tracks **8,925 clips with one policy** at 50 Hz ([arXiv 2506.14770](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14770v2)). Disney shipped the per-clip design and paid for it: "each episodic motion needs its own policy", about 22 GPU-days each, and future work "explicitly includes single-policy learning for multiple skills" ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). Fine-tuning one locomotion network per new skill forgets the old ones, because PPO "will prioritize the current situation at the expense of forgetting previously acquired skills" ([arXiv 2311.06828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06828v1.pdf)).

The cause is state-distribution mismatch at the handoff: a policy only knows the states its own reference produced, so the first frames after a hot-swap are out of distribution. BeyondMimic's robot "is stable once a gait orbit is established, but tends to stumble at the start and end of motions" ([arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4)); Boston Dynamics warns that interrupting a dance mid-motion "may cause falls" and that moves controlling the same tracks "cannot run simultaneously" ([Boston Dynamics Choreographer docs](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/choreography/choreographer)). Where expert networks are blended on hardware, the gate was trained jointly with them: MELA's gating network "constantly blends multiple DNNs" on a real quadruped but learned to do so with the experts ([arXiv 2012.05810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05810)); LooperMuscle gates per joint group inside one actor ([arXiv 2608.00820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00820)).

The resolution keeps your goal and changes the unit. Make controls replaceable **data** tracked by one policy trained with a standard reward template every time. Replacing a control then means replacing a clip and, at most, re-running the one standard training job with the new clip in the motion set (BeyondMimic's "shared formulation and training setup" ([arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4))); Disney's **30-minute preview at about 1,500 iterations** makes that loop tolerable ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). AMOR goes further: one policy conditioned on reward weights that are tuned after training, on the real robot, in about one day versus five days of training ([arXiv 2505.23708](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23708v1)). A separately trained network is justified only for a motion the shared tracker demonstrably cannot learn (KungfuBot's single-motion policies for maximum dynamism ([arXiv 2506.12851](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12851v2))), and it must be entered and exited through a trained transition state, as ToddlerBot does by sampling the walk policy's initial state from the grasping policy's end pose ([arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v4)).

Motion matching does not change this verdict for the duck. Epic's own admission is that "weights cannot fix poor coverage" ([Epic docs, Motion Matching](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine)), For Honor ran weekly mocap sessions to fill gaps ([Game Watch Impress](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/749136.html)), and Disney's robot uses no motion matching at all ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). With a library of tens of clips, a state machine with aliases and a cost-biased selector is cheaper and more predictable.

### Governor and self-correction: phase gates, similarity thresholds, additive offsets

The working governors are small. Switch's scheduler fires on initialisation, a user target change, end of reference, or a similarity crossing: below threshold A it attaches directly, above threshold B it **emergency-stops**, in between it scores top-k candidates; a 500 N push during a kick is recovered only with the scheduler active ([arXiv 2604.14834](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14834v1)). Disney's transitions into walking are phase-aware, transitions out wait for the next double-support phase, and a "motion stop" forces standing and "freezes setpoints with high gains after 0.5 s" ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). Choreographer's track ownership (Legs, Body, Arm, Gripper, Lights) is the rule that keeps additive head and show layers from contending with the leg track ([Boston Dynamics Choreographer docs](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/choreography/choreographer)). Unity's BehaviourPuppet is a complete supervisor template: unpin when an impulse exceeds `collisionResistance`, knock out when tracking error exceeds `knockOutDistance`, regain pins at `regainPinSpeed`, get up when hip velocity drops below `maxGetUpVelocity` ([PuppetMaster, BehaviourPuppet](http://www.root-motion.com/puppetmasterdox/html/page10.html)).

Self-correction belongs in two places on purpose. Inside the tracker, by training with pushes, terrain and sensor delay, which is what already works on the duck and on BD-X, whose policy "departs from the reference trajectory and contact schedule to recover" ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). And in a thin classical layer between governor and tracker that is always on and needs no retraining. NimbRo's igus platform, on "pure proportional position control" with only joint encoders and a 6-axis IMU, adds fused-pitch and fused-roll driven offsets (hip angle, support-foot angle, CoM shift, a "virtual slope", gait timing) and walked onto an unexpected 1.5 cm step, recovering from a lateral push "within 2.5 s" ([Allgeuer and Behnke, arXiv 1809.10712](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.10712)). Missura's capture steps on NimbRo-OP2 run at **100 Hz in 0.12 ms per iteration on a 1.3 GHz core**, with the CoM taken as the hip midpoint rotated by IMU attitude and balance "corrected only by modifying step size and timing", cutting hand-push falls from 22 of 58 to 18 of 97 ([arXiv 2011.02793](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.02793v1)). SURENA-Mini sets ankle, hip and arm targets from one PD law on capture-point error ([arXiv 1710.10598](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.10598)). All of this fits in the 20 ms budget beside an MLP, and the trigger variable in every case is a divergent balance quantity (capture point, orbital energy, fused tilt plus rate), never raw tilt alone.

"Blending back" is then implicit: the offsets decay with the error (Allgeuer's integrators have a chosen half-life), and the next nominal step is planned from the corrected state. Do not implement recovery as a separate RL policy that is cross-faded in; no hardware result supports action-space cross-fading between independently trained policies.

Terrain follows from the sensing. With IMU, joint positions and two foot switches there is no foothold to warp a clip to, so the duck's terrain tolerance comes from rough-terrain randomisation (the Playground ships `scene_mjx_rough_terrain.xml` ([Open_Duck_Playground](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Playground))), from the IMU offset layer, and from Rhoban's trick of delaying the trajectory clock on a late touchdown detected by foot pressure ([arXiv 2402.00612](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00612)). Walk These Ways crossed stairs and curbs while trained on flat ground ([ar5iv 2212.03238](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)), so blind robustness is realistic.

Measure the governor the way the field does: falls per push session, mean absolute joint tracking error per control (BD-X reports **0.027 to 0.123 rad** across its motions ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1))), and transition success rate per (control A to control B) pair, because transitions are where every surveyed system failed.

## The duck's life layer: head, timing and anticipation are free; leg style must be trained in (LIFE)

Everything in this section rides on top of the tracker and never touches a leg joint directly. That is the split Disney, Anki and Boston Dynamics all use, and it is the reason "life" can be iterated daily while the tracker is retrained rarely.

Disney's runtime animation engine has three layers: "(1) A looping background animation (eye blinks, antenna motion). (2) Operator-triggered clips from an artist library, blended in and out. (3) Joystick-driven offsets to head and torso pose, or to walking velocities" ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)). Olaf keeps its low-inertia show functions (eyes, eyebrows, jaw) under classical control and "out of the policy" ([arXiv 2512.16705](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16705)). The duck already does this for the head: after inference the operator's head command is added as an offset to the policy's head outputs, `head_motor_targets = last_commands[3:] + motor_targets[5:9]`, at a 4-times lower servo gain ([Open_Duck_Mini_Runtime, v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Mini_Runtime/blob/v2/scripts/v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py)), and the training reward "explicitly removes neck, head and antenna joints from the imitated joint set" ([custom_rewards.py](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Playground/blob/main/playground/open_duck_mini_v2/custom_rewards.py)). The head is the duck's free expression channel; BD-X is also 5+5+4 = 14 joints, so its ownership map transfers one-to-one ([arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204v1)).

Two hardware facts cap how far the life layer may go. First, physics erodes style on purpose: Olaf's foot-impact reward cut sound by **13.5 dB** but "smooths out subtle reference details, such as the mid-swing dip of the foot", and "small inconsistencies, such as rough foot impacts or jitter, can break the character's lifelike appearance" ([arXiv 2512.16705](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16705)); AMOR found "smoothness terms typically need to be weighted much higher for satisfactory results on real hardware" ([arXiv 2505.23708](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23708v1)). Jitter and loud footfalls kill the illusion faster than missing style, which makes the sim-to-real work below a LIFE investment as much as a REALISM one. Second, expression must not exceed capability: in Apple's ELEGNT study expressive motion scored **56.16 versus 28.77** overall, but gestures "seemed 'fake' when they conflicted with the robot's capabilities" and added nothing on function-oriented tasks ([arXiv 2501.12493](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12493)). Keep expression heavy in social and idle moments and light during precision stepping.

Motion signals for a robot also need to be smoother than for a CG character: Ribeiro and Paiva argue for at least C2 continuity, with their Nutty Motion Filter saturating velocity, acceleration and jerk and offering slow-in, slow-out and optional controlled overshoot as character parameters ([Ribeiro and Paiva 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02898)). That filter is the same object as Unreal's inertializer with clamps, sitting in command space on the head channel.

The precedents for the tooling are consistent. Anki built "software inside of Maya" so animators could animate "an actual robot" with four motors synced live ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/cozmo-ai-robot-brings-wall-e-to-life/)), stored animations as FlatBuffers keyframes, generated the face procedurally from 43 parameters, and ran emotions as decaying values driven by reactions ([PyCozmo docs](https://pycozmo.readthedocs.io/en/latest/external/offboard_functions.html)). The Robot Engine drove a 25-DoF InMoov from Unity's Mecanim state machine over serial, clamping each joint per servo, with no feedback and no physics ([Bartneck et al., RO-MAN 2015](https://www.bartneck.de/publications/2015/unity/index.html)), which is a LIFE-only pipeline and the right scope for the head channel but not the legs. No public, primary-source example of Unreal's animation runtime driving a walking robot was found; use Unreal or Blender for authoring and previewing limits, and port the runtime ideas.

## Sim-to-real: the actuator is the gap, and register 85 is the prime suspect

Before training or animating on the duck, the simulation must reproduce three things your own logs already show: real servos measure about **1.48 times stiffer** than the model on a static scale test, a fast recovery step tracks at **2.1° mean / 7.9° max on hardware versus 1.4° / 4.9° in sim**, and quiet standing transfers while a learned recovery step amplifies tilt (project measurements, from the research brief). Across every small servo biped with published sim-to-real detail, the dominant gap is the actuator (torque ceiling, friction, acceleration and velocity limits, temperature) with latency second; Tan et al. named "inaccurate actuator models and lack of latency modeling" as "two major causes of the reality gap" on Minitaur ([Tan et al., RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)), and ToddlerBot found that "the actuation model does not consider motor temperature and is less accurate when close to the performance limit" ([ToddlerBot, arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v3)). The duck's symptom profile, static fine and transients wrong, points the same way.

### The STS3215 acceleration register

The single most important finding of this research is an undocumented acceleration profile in the STS3215 firmware. A BAM contributor identifying the 12 V variant reported: "Out of the box, the servo limits acceleration to about 10–28 rad/s² through an undocumented register" (**register 85, "maximum acceleration", factory value 50**), that the shipped BAM STS3215 model does not include it, and that writing 85 = 0 disables it "but only after register 41 (acceleration) is also written", both RAM registers that reset at power-up; with the profile disabled the no-load response reached about 110 rad/s² ([Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)). A second contributor confirmed the mechanism, measured 6.0 rad/s² on a balanced rod at cap 50, found the cap unit-specific (about 22 rad/s² on one bare horn versus 7.2 rad/s² on another, "cause unknown"), measured only 29 to 38 rad/s² with the profile off, and showed that modelling the profile in the simulated goal cut held-out error from **18.1 to 7.1 mrad**; LeRobot writes 254 to both registers on every connect ([Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)). Pollen's rustypot driver recently fixed register 85 to one byte, noting the older memory table stops at address 69 ([pollen-robotics/rustypot PR #129](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/rustypot/pull/129)).

Why this fits the duck's symptoms (inferred): a 6 to 28 rad/s² cap barely touches quiet standing or a 1.2 Hz-filtered walk, but a recovery step needs tens of rad/s². The BAM model, and therefore the Playground's position actuator, was fitted with the profile off, so the simulated servo is faster than the real one in exactly the transients where tilt amplification appears. Tracking error 1.5 times higher on hardware while static stiffness is higher is the signature of a rate or acceleration limit, not a gain error. Nobody has yet checked which values the Open Duck runtime writes to registers 41 and 85 at boot; until that is known the sim is probably strictly faster than the robot in transients.

### What else is known about this servo class

BAM (Rhoban, ICRA 2025) is the best-documented identification route: a rigid pendulum bench, a DC-motor voltage model with back-EMF, and six friction models from Coulomb-viscous (M1, the MuJoCo default) through Stribeck, load-dependent and quadratic terms (M6), fitted with CMA-ES on about 100 logs of 6 s each, 75/25 split, converging in under 5 minutes; the best model cut trajectory error versus M1 by 2.93 times on an MX-64 and 2.02 times on an MX-106 ([BAM paper, arXiv 2410.08650](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08650v4); [Rhoban/bam](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam)). For the 12 V STS3215 the held-out error was **20.8 mrad for M1 versus 10.3 to 10.5 mrad for M4 to M6**, and the 7.4 V parameters "do not transfer to the 12 V variant" ([Rhoban/bam PR #20](https://github.com/Rhoban/bam/pull/20)). Open Duck's sim2real doc states that BAM-identified STS3215 parameters are exported to MuJoCo as damping, kp, frictionloss, armature and forcerange ([Open_Duck_Mini docs/sim2real.md](https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Mini/blob/main/docs/sim2real.md)). Note on this repository's gains: the model the duck was trained on, `open_duck_mini_v2_backlash.xml`, uses `<position kp="17.11" forcerange="-3.23 3.23"/>`; the upstream non-backlash `open_duck_mini_v2.xml` uses kp 13.37 with the same force range (checked 2026-11-05). Any fit must be compared against the backlash model's 17.11.

The torque ceiling is the second actuator issue. The sim allows ±3.23 N·m per joint while the catalog rates the STS3215 at 19 kg·cm, which is 1.86 N·m ([orobot.io](https://orobot.io/o/program/BROKER-2/open-duck-mini)); a community build found the knee hitting the sim ceiling 14 to 16% of the time and, after retraining at 1.86 N·m, walking speed under that limit rose from **0.057 to 0.130 m/s** ([soyeon24 build log](https://github.com/soyeon24/open-duck-mini-v2-build)). Whether 3.23 is BAM's pre-friction electrical stall torque or an overestimate cannot be resolved without a stall test (inferred). Backlash measured about 0.87° on a bench against a datasheet limit of 0.5°, growing under load ([Robo9 STS3215 test](https://robonine.com/testing-of-feetech-sts3215-servomotor-backlash-repeatability-and-torque/)). The register map worth dumping: P/D/I at 21/22/23 (defaults 32, 32, 0), EEPROM max torque 16, RAM torque limit 48, goal acceleration 41, protection torque 34 (default 20%), overload threshold 36 (default 80%) ([STS3215 register reference](https://github.com/commanderfun/STS3215/blob/main/REGISTER_REFERENCE.md)). Supply voltage scales torque linearly in the DC-motor model, so a 2S pack at 8.4 V full to 6.4 V near empty spans roughly 0.86 to 1.14 times the 7.4 V fit (arithmetic, not a measurement).

Latency is next. Tan et al. found that instantaneous simulation "makes the stability region of a feedback controller in simulation much larger than its implementation on hardware. For this reason, we often see a feedback policy learned in simulation starts to oscillate, diverge and ultimately fail in the real world", and randomised latency 0 to 40 ms and control step 3 to 20 ms ([Tan et al., RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)); an Open Duck MJX port tests push recovery with 0 to 40 ms of actuation and IMU latency ([SeanEZhou/openduck](https://github.com/SeanEZhou/openduck)). No published end-to-end latency exists for 14 STS3215 on one bus from a Pi Zero 2 W in Python; a wire-time estimate is about 1.1 ms per sync-write and 1.3 ms per sync-read plus firmware turnarounds and several ms of Python overhead, so 3 to 8 ms per cycle is plausible and must be measured (inferred). BeyondMimic's sensitivity curve is a useful scale: an injected delay of 2 ms raised error, 5 ms caused one failure, 10 ms failed two of three trials on a G1 ([arXiv 2508.08241](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v4)).

The IMU and the mass model complete the picture. In IMU mode the BNO055's on-chip fusion was reported to hold yaw static below about 3 °/s and only track above 5 to 10 °/s while the raw gyro stayed usable ([kriswiner/BNO055 issue #12](https://github.com/kriswiner/BNO055/issues/12)); if the fusion deadband ignores slow tilt, projected gravity lags or freezes during a slow lean (inferred). Tan weighed every link, estimated inertia from shape, then randomised mass only ±20% but inertia ±50% "because it is estimated based on a uniform density assumption" ([Tan et al., RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)); the duck's **2.107 kg model versus a 1.5 to 1.8 kg estimate** is a 17 to 40% discrepancy, and a model that is 28% heavy expects 28% more gravity torque in a lean, which combined with a stiffer real servo produces over-correction (inferred). Contact is the least documented: Tan randomised friction 0.5 to 1.25, and no source tunes MuJoCo `solref` to a measured drop test for any small biped.

### Verification: replay, fit a few parameters, then narrow the randomisation

The 2025 to 2026 literature agrees on the loop: log real rollouts, replay or match them in sim, fit a handful of parameters with CMA-ES or learn a small residual, then fine-tune the policy in the corrected sim. ASAP records real-robot data, trains a delta-action model that "outputs corrective actions that are added to the recorded real-world actions", freezes it in the simulator, fine-tunes, and deploys without it ([ASAP, arXiv 2502.01143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01143)). The 2026 simulator-adaptation paper needs only **64 trajectories of 4 s (about 256 s)** of proprioception, matches per-joint distributions with a Wasserstein cost that needs no time alignment, and cut a biped's drift from about 1.2 m to 0.2 m after fine-tuning ([arXiv 2604.11090](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11090)). ToddlerBot fitted nine actuator parameters from back-driving, spin-down and a chirp, reached "average tracking error of 1.3°", and transferred the policy to a second robot without re-identification ([ToddlerBot, arXiv 2502.00893](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00893v3)). On randomisation, Tan's rule stands: "dynamics randomization trades optimality for robustness", so use narrow ranges for measured quantities and wide ones only for what drifts (motor strength, control step, latency, battery) ([Tan et al., RSS 2018](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss14/p10.pdf)). The Playground currently randomises kp by 1.1 to 1.8 times, which contains the 1.48 factor but centres the policy on a value that is probably wrong; that the recovery step still fails suggests the gap is not kp alone (inferred).

Static stiffness and stall (1 h) | Scale on a lever arm | Torque versus commanded deflection at 2°, 5°, 10°, 20° both directions, 2 s holds; a 20 s hold for stall and protection-torque fold-back | Slope outside the hysteresis band is kp; band width is friction plus backlash (expect about 0.9°, documented by Robo9). Standing push battery | Known mass on a string, or the scale | Calibrated pushes at 3 magnitudes and 2 directions on hardware and in sim; tilt peak and settle time | The real-world counterpart of the existing `sim/fusion/runs/passive_push` sweep; last gate before training any new recovery skill |

Two steps above are the whole difference between a sim that flatters and a sim that predicts: step 0 (the register dump) and step 8 (the replay gate). Everything in sections three and four assumes step 8 passes; a tracker trained against a simulated servo that accelerates four times faster than the real one will keep learning recovery steps the hardware cannot execute.

## Conclusion

The research changes the project's framing in two ways. First, the game engines are worth copying for their separation of concerns and their weight-everywhere discipline, not for their mechanisms: on a robot with an IMU and encoders, warping and foot IK have no foothold to target, motion matching has no clip coverage to search, and Euphoria-style behaviours already exist as capture-point PD offsets that cost 0.12 ms per tick. What survives is the shape: measure once, select from data, blend with one inertializer in command space, ground through a balance controller that is allowed to disobey, and keep every layer's contribution a 0-to-1 weight. Second, "individually replaceable components" is achievable, but the replaceable unit is a clip plus a command vector, and the one thing that must stay shared is the tracker. That is not a compromise of the design; it is what makes "change one animation without retraining everything" true in practice, because a clip edit is a data edit and a new leg skill is one standard re-run previewable in 30 minutes.

The sharpest practical implication is that life and realism are not sequential phases. Olaf's team found that jitter and loud footfalls break the illusion of life faster than any missing style, and the duck's own recovery-step mismatch traces most plausibly to a firmware acceleration cap that nobody has read back yet. Reading two registers and running the hanging-leg replay is therefore the first animation task, not the last engineering one; the head channel, attention engine and anticipation clips can be built in parallel because they never touch the tracker, and they are where this robot's character will be decided.
